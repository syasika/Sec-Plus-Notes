# Introduction to Threat Actors

## Overview

- **Definition**: An entity responsible for an event that impacts the safety of another entity. Also called a **malicious actor**.

- **Threat Actor Attributes**: Describes characteristics of the attacker, useful for categorizing their motivation and ultimate goal.
  - **Why is this attack happening?**
  - Is it directed or random?

## Attributes of Threat Actors

### Location
- **Internal**: The attacker is inside the organization (e.g., an employee).
- **External**: The attacker is outside trying to gain access.

### Resources/Funding
- **No money**: Limited resources, simpler attacks.
- **Extensive funding**: Large amount of money available, complex and numerous attacks.

### Level of Sophistication/Capability
- **Low sophistication**: Runs pre-made scripts or automated scans without deep understanding.
- **High sophistication**: Can write custom malware and understand underlying systems.
- **Medium sophistication**: Somewhere in between.

## Motivations of Threat Actors

- **Data exfiltration (stealing data)**
- **Espionage (gathering corporate or national intelligence)**
- **Service disruption (DDoS, taking down systems)**
- **Blackmail (ranging from extortion to ransomware)**
- **Financial gain (direct monetary theft, credit card fraud)**
- **Philosophical/political beliefs (hacktivism)**
- **Ethical reasons (moral disagreement)**
- **Revenge (disgruntled employees or former employees)**
- **Disruption/chaos (pure anarchy)**
- **War (cyber warfare between nations)**

## Types of Threat Actors

### 1. Nation States
- **Location**: External entity.
- **Purpose**: Government and national security interests.
- **Possible Motivations**:
  - Data exfiltration
  - Philosophical/political reasons
  - Revenge
  - Disruption
  - War
- **Resources**: Constant attacks, massive resources. Often referred to as an **Advanced Persistent Threat (APT)** due to their continuous, well-resourced attacks.
- **Sophistication**: Highest sophistication. Can afford the most advanced developers and attack types.
- **Targets**: Military control, utilities, financial control.
- **Example**: **Stuxnet worm** (created by USA and Israel to destroy Iranian nuclear centrifuges).

### 2. Unskilled Attackers
- **Capability**: Runs pre-made scripts without any real knowledge of what's happening.
- **Motivation**: Often "the hunt" itself, disruption, data exfiltration, or philosophical beliefs.
- **Location**: Can be internal or external but usually external.
- **Sophistication**: Not very sophisticated. Limited resources, no formal funding.

### 3. Hacktivist
- **Definition**: A hacker with a clear purpose.
- **Motivation**: Motivated by philosophy, political beliefs, revenge, disruption, etc.
- **Location**: Often an external entity but could potentially infiltrate to also be an **Insider Threat**.
- **Sophistication**: Can be remarkably sophisticated. Uses very specific hacks (e.g., DoS, website defacing, private document release).
- **Funding**: May be limited. Some organizations have fundraising options.

### 4. Insider Threat
- **Definition**: Someone within the organization with access to sensitive information.
- **Motivation**: Motivated by revenge, financial gain, disruption, etc.
- **Resources**: Extensive resources. Uses institutional knowledge against the organization.
- **Location**: An internal entity.
- **Sophistication**: Medium level of sophistication. Has **institutional knowledge** and can target vulnerable systems effectively.

### 5. Organized Crime
- **Definition**: Professional criminals.
- **Motivation**: Primarily financial gain (financial theft).
- **Location**: Almost always an external entity.
- **Sophistication**: Very sophisticated. "Best hacking money can buy."
- **Structure**: Corporate-like structure with roles for hacking, exploiting vulnerabilities, selling data, and handling customer support.
- **Resources**: Lots of capital to fund hacking efforts.

### 6. Shadow IT
- **Definition**: Working around the internal IT organization by building their own infrastructure (e.g., using personal credit cards for cloud services).
- **Challenge**: Information Technology can put up roadblocks, but Shadow IT is unencumbered.
- **Resources**: Limited resources (company budget, personal funds).
- **Sophistication**: Medium sophistication. May not have IT training or knowledge.

# Key Takeaways for Exam Preparation

1. **Understand the Core Definition**: A "threat actor" is simply the entity causing a security event. Analyze their attributes and motivations to predict and defend against their actions.

2. **Categorization is Crucial**:
   - **Define each type**.
   - **Identify their typical location (internal/external)**.
   - **Describe their resource levels**.
   - **Assess their sophistication/capability**.
   - **List their primary motivations**.

3. **Key Distinctions for Each Category**:
   - **Nation States (APTs)**: Highest resources and sophistication, often long-term, targeted attacks for geopolitical reasons (e.g., Stuxnet).
   - **Organized Crime**: High resources and sophistication, profit-driven, often with a corporate-like structure.
   - **Hacktivists**: Motivated by ideology, can be highly sophisticated but often have limited funding.
   - **Insider Threats**: Internal access is their biggest advantage; institutional knowledge makes them dangerous. *Vetting during hiring is critical*.
   - **Shadow IT**: Internal, often unintentional threat due to bypassing official IT for perceived efficiency, limited IT/security knowledge but can innovate, creating significant unmanaged risks.
   - **Unskilled Attackers**: Low sophistication, use readily available tools, often just "for the thrill" or simple disruption, target "low-hanging fruit."

4. **Motivation Drives Strategy**:
   - Financial gain? Focus on data encryption, fraud detection, strong access controls.
   - Disruption? Focus on redundancy, DDoS protection, incident response.
   - Espionage? Focus on data exfiltration prevention, network segmentation, strong authentication.

5. **Resources and Sophistication Dictate Attack Methods**:
   - High resources/sophistication (Nation States, Organized Crime) will use custom malware, zero-days, complex multi-stage attacks. Your defenses need to be equally advanced and layered.
   - Low resources/sophistication (Unskilled, some Shadow IT) will rely on known vulnerabilities, social engineering, and easily available scripts. Basic security hygiene is often effective.

6. **The Summary Table Is Your Best Friend**: Memorize this table. It condenses all critical attributes for each threat actor type into an easily digestible format, perfect for quick recall during an exam.

7. **Real-World Examples**: Remember examples like Stuxnet to illustrate the capabilities of nation-state actors. Think about common ransomware attacks as examples of organized crime.

By focusing on these points, you should be well-prepared to analyze and respond to questions about different threat actors in a cybersecurity exam.
