# Wireless Attacks (Deauthentication & RF Jamming)

## 1. Wireless Deauthentication Attacks

### Introduction to Deauthentication

**Scenario:** Imagine you are on a wireless network, and suddenly your device gets disconnected without any warning or message. This happens repeatedly, and there's no way for you to stop it.

**Definition:** **Wireless deauthentication** is a significant **Denial of Service (DoS)** attack that actively disconnects users from a wireless network, preventing them from accessing network services.

### 802.11 Management Frames

#### Core Vulnerability

Deauthentication attacks exploit weaknesses in the 802.11 management frames, which are essential for making wireless networks function but are invisible to regular users.

**Purpose of Management Frames:** These frames handle:
- Finding access points (APs).
- Managing Quality of Service (QoS).
- **Association/Disassociation** with an AP.
- Authentication/deauthentication.

#### The Flaw

In the original 802.11 wireless standards, management frames were sent "in the clear" (unencrypted) and lacked authentication or validation. As a result, attackers can easily manipulate these frames to cause problems for users on the network.

### Example of an Unprotected Management Frame (Wireshark)

**Demonstration:** A Wireshark packet capture shows an 802.11 Association Request frame.
- **Observation:** All information within the frame (receiver/destination/transmitter/source MAC addresses, BSSID, SSID ("pmn"), supported rates, capabilities) is visible and unencrypted.
- **Implication:** Anyone within range can capture this information and use it for attacks.

### Deauthentication Attack Demonstration

**Goal:** To disconnect a specific client (iPhone) from a wireless network.

**Prerequisites for Attacker:**
- Knowledge of the client's MAC address (STATION).
- Knowledge of the Access Point's MAC address (BSSID).
- A wireless adapter capable of monitor mode and packet injection (e.g., on a Linux device like Kali Linux).

**Tools Used:**

```markdown
- `airodump-ng wlan0mon`: To discover nearby APs (BSSIDs) and connected clients (STATION MACs).
- `aireplay-ng -0 100 -a [AP_MAC] -c [CLIENT_MAC] wlan0mon`: The command to execute the deauthentication attack.
    * `-0`: Specifies a deauthentication attack.
    * `100`: Number of deauthentication frames to send (can be 0 for continuous).
    * `-a [AP_MAC]`: Specifies the target Access Point's BSSID.
    * `-c [CLIENT_MAC]`: Specifies the target client's MAC address.
    * `wlan0mon`: The attacker's wireless interface in monitor mode.
```

**Attack Execution & Result:**
- The `aireplay-ng` command is executed.
- The target iPhone immediately loses its connection to the "pmn" wireless network.
- As long as deauthentication frames are continuously sent, the device cannot reconnect, effectively creating a DoS condition for that client.

### Protecting Against Deauthentication Attacks

The IEEE 802.11 committee recognized this as a significant security concern and included security updates with the **802.11ac** standard and newer versions.
- **Updates:** Some important management frames are now encrypted by default (e.g., Disassociate, deauthenticate, channel switch announcements).
- **Still Unencrypted:** Not everything is encrypted. Frames essential for initial network discovery and connection remain unencrypted (e.g., Beacons, probes, authentication, association frames).

## 2. Radio Frequency (RF) Jamming

### Introduction to RF Jamming

**Definition:** Another type of **Denial of Service (DoS)** attack that targets the physical layer (radio frequency), preventing wireless communication by transmitting interfering signals.

**Goal:** To make it impossible for devices to communicate on a specific wireless network.

**Mechanism:** The attacker transmits a strong, noisy signal on the same frequency as the legitimate wireless network. This decreases the **signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)** at the receiving device, making communication impossible.

#### Unintentional Interference vs. Intentional Jamming

- **Unintentional Interference:** Sometimes RF interference isn't malicious (e.g., microwave ovens, fluorescent lights).
- **Intentional Jamming:** Occurs when someone deliberately sends out signals to disrupt a wireless network.

### Types of Wireless Jamming

**Various Methods to Create Noise:**
- **Constant, random bits:** Transmitting a continuous stream of meaningless data.
- **Constant, legitimate frames:** Flooding the network with valid-looking but overwhelming traffic.
- **Data sent at random times:** Sending random data and legitimate frames intermittently, making troubleshooting harder.
- **Reactive jamming:** The jammer remains silent until it detects a legitimate transmission, then starts jamming.

### Locating a Wireless Jammer ("Fox Hunting")

**Proximity Requirement:** Similar to deauthentication attacks, RF jamming requires the attacker to be physically close to the target network to be effective.

**Process:** Finding the source of a jam is often called "fox hunting" by amateur radio operators.
- **Equipment for Fox Hunting:**
    * **Directional antenna:** Used to determine the direction from which the strongest signal is coming. By moving around and noting signal strength, one can triangulate the source.
    * **Attenuator:** A device used to lower the signal strength. As you get closer to the jammer, the signal becomes very strong. An attenuator reduces this strength, allowing for more precise directional readings without overwhelming the receiver.

## Takeaways for an Exam

### Deauthentication Attack Fundamentals
- Understand that a deauthentication attack is a *wireless DoS*.
- It primarily disconnects users from a wireless network.
- Know the unencrypted nature of 802.11 management frames in older standards.
- Tools used: `airodump-ng` (discovery), `aireplay-ng` (attack).
- Identify necessary information for the attack: AP BSSID and Client MAC address.

### 802.11 Management Frame Security
- Recall that original 802.11 standards sent management frames "in the clear," allowing manipulation.
- Identify which standard (802.11ac and newer) introduced encryption for important management frames to mitigate this.
- Know that not all management frames are encrypted (e.g., beacons, probes, authentication, association frames).

### RF Jamming Concepts
- Recognize RF jamming as another form of *wireless DoS* affecting all users on a given frequency.
- Understand the underlying principle: decreasing the signal-to-noise ratio by transmitting interfering signals.
- Differentiate between intentional jamming and unintentional interference (e.g., microwaves, fluorescent lights).
- Know general types of jamming (constant noise, legitimate frames, random, reactive).

### Mitigation and Location of Jammers
- Understand that both deauthentication and RF jamming require the attacker to be in *physical proximity*.
- For RF jamming, know the concept of "fox hunting" and basic tools involved: a directional antenna for finding the source direction and an attenuator for refining location by managing signal strength.

### Distinctions
- Clearly distinguish between a deauthentication attack (protocol-level DoS targeting specific clients or all clients via AP spoofing) and RF jamming (physical-layer DoS targeting all communication on a frequency).
- Deauth targets the protocol handshake; jamming targets the physical medium.
