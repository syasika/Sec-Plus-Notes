# Denial of Service (DoS) & Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)

## Overview

### Definition
Denial of Service (DoS) is an attack where an attacker deliberately forces a service to fail, making it unavailable to legitimate users. A Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) involves multiple compromised devices launching attacks on the same target.

### Types of DoS Attacks

#### Intentional DoS
- **Overload the Service**
  - The attacker floods the service with traffic or requests, consuming all available resources such as CPU, memory, and network bandwidth.
- **Exploit a Design Failure or Vulnerability**
  - Identifying weaknesses in system software or configuration to crash or freeze the service.
    - **Mitigation**: Keep systems patched and up-to-date.
- **Cause Unavailability of Information**
  - **Competitive Advantage**: An organization might launch a DoS attack against a competitor to disrupt their services.
  - **Smokescreen for Another Attack**: A DoS can draw attention away from another, more subtle attack.

#### Unintentional DoS ("Friendly" DoS)
- Occurs unintentionally due to misconfigurations or accidents.
  - **Network DoS (Layer 2 Loop)**
    - Connecting two switches without Spanning Tree Protocol enabled creates a broadcast storm, potentially bringing down the network.
  - **Bandwidth DoS**
    - A user downloading a large file over limited bandwidth can consume all available resources.
  - **Physical Damage**
    - Water damage to data center equipment due to a broken water line.

## Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS)

A DDoS attack involves an "army" of compromised computers, often referred to as a botnet, attacking a target simultaneously.

### Characteristics
- **Multiple Devices Bombard the Target**
  - Multiple devices send requests, overwhelming the server or network infrastructure.
- **Attackers Use Botnets**
  - Thousands or millions of computers infected with malware are controlled by an attacker. Examples include:
    - **Zeus Botnet**: Infected over 3.6 million PCs at its peak.
    - **Coordinated Attack**: A single command initiates a simultaneous attack from all infected machines.

### Asymmetric Threat
- The attacker, with limited resources, leverages the vast botnet to overwhelm organizations with far greater infrastructure and bandwidth.

## DDoS Reflection and Amplification

A technique used in DDoS attacks to turn small attacks into large ones by exploiting common protocols.

### Reflection
- **Internet Services Against the Victim**
  - Attackers exploit protocols that send larger responses than initial requests.
- **Protocols with Little Authentication or Checks**
  - Examples: NTP, DNS, ICMP

### Amplification
- **Common Protocol Abuse Example: DNS Amplification**
  - A small query requesting "ANY" information for a domain can receive a large response (e.g., amplification factor of ~86x).

## DDoS Amplification Example (Visualized)

1. **Botnet Command and Control (C&C)**
   - The attacker sends a command to the botnet.
2. **Botnet Initiates Queries**
   - Infected machines send small DNS queries to multiple open DNS resolvers, with source IP addresses spoofed as the target web server's IP address.
3. **DNS Resolvers Respond**
   - Open DNS resolvers process the small queries and send large responses to the spoofed source IP (the target web server).
4. **Target Overwhelmed**
   - The web server receives a massive flood of amplified DNS responses from numerous resolvers, overwhelming its bandwidth and resources.

## Exam Takeaways

1. **Define DoS and DDoS**
   - **DoS**: Single attacker/source aiming to make a service unavailable.
   - **DDoS**: Multiple attackers/sources (botnet) coordinating to make a service unavailable.
2. **Understand the Goals of DoS/DDoS**
   - Overload resources, exploit vulnerabilities, gain competitive advantage, or serve as a smokescreen for other attacks.
3. **Recognize Unintentional DoS Scenarios**
   - Layer 2 loops (lack of Spanning Tree Protocol), excessive legitimate bandwidth usage, physical infrastructure failures.
4. **Key Component of DDoS: Botnets**
   - Networks of compromised computers controlled by an attacker allowing for coordinated large-scale attacks.
5. **DDoS as an Asymmetric Threat**
   - A single attacker with limited resources can leverage a botnet to overwhelm a target with significant resources.
6. **Reflection and Amplification are Advanced DDoS Techniques**
   - **Reflection**: Sending traffic to intermediaries that reflect it to the victim.
   - **Amplification**: Using protocols where a small request generates a much larger response, multiplying the attack's impact (common protocols: NTP, DNS, ICMP).
7. **DNS Amplification Attack Mechanism**
   - Attacker spoofs the victim’s IP address.
   - Bots send small DNS queries to open DNS resolvers using the spoofed IP.
   - Resolvers send large responses to the spoofed IP (the victim), overwhelming it.
8. **General Mitigation**
   - Keep systems patched to prevent exploitation of vulnerabilities.
   - Implement Spanning Tree Protocol to prevent Layer 2 loops.
   - Properly configure DNS resolvers to prevent amplification attacks.
   - Utilize DDoS mitigation services.
