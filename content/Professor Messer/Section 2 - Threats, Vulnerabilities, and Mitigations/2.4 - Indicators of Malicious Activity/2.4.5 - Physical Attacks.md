# Physical Attacks

## Introduction to Physical Attacks
- **Definition**: Security concerns unrelated to digital technology (operating systems, software vulnerabilities, network protocols). Focuses on direct, in-person manipulation or access to physical assets.
- **Old-School Security**: This type of security doesn't involve keyboards, mice, or command lines. It's about protecting the physical infrastructure.
- **Core Principle**: If an attacker gains **physical access** to a server or computing device, they generally have **full control**, bypassing digital security measures.
    - An operating system cannot stop an in-person, physical attack.
- **Importance**: Just as there are many ways to circumvent digital security, there are many ways to attack physical security. A physical approach to security must always be considered as part of a comprehensive defense strategy.
- **Analogy**: "Door locks keep out the honest people."
    - This implies that basic physical controls are easily circumvented by determined attackers.
    - Requires implementing **additional tools** and layers of security to maintain physical integrity.

## Types of Physical Attacks

### Brute Force (Physical Version)
- **Description**: Directly pushing through an obstruction using physical strength, tools, or destructive means. It's the physical counterpart to a password brute-force attack.
- **Concept**: "Brawn beats brains" in this context; raw power to overcome physical barriers.
- **Examples**:
    - Kicking in a door.
    - Breaking a window.
    - Cutting through a fence.
    - Forcing open a locked cabinet or server rack.
- **Defense/Evaluation**:
    - Organizations should regularly **check their physical security** measures.
    - Test the strength and integrity of **windows** and **doors**.
    - Identify potential weak points in the building's perimeter.

### RFID Cloning
- **RFID (Radio-Frequency Identification)**: A technology widely used for physical access control.
    - Examples: **Access badges** (ID cards), **key fobs** for doors, building entry, etc.
- **Prevalence**: RFID technology is ubiquitous in modern access control systems.
- **Vulnerability**: RFID duplicators (cloners) are inexpensive and easily obtainable.
    - Can be purchased for less than $50 USD on platforms like Amazon.
- **Duplication Process**: Extremely fast, taking only seconds.
    - **Read one card**: An attacker can use a hidden RFID reader to scan an employee's badge just by brushing past them (e.g., on public transport, in a crowded hallway).
    - **Copy to another**: The scanned data is then written to a blank RFID card, creating a duplicate.
- **Real-world Impact**: A cloned RFID card grants the attacker the same physical access as the legitimate cardholder.
- **Defense**: Implement **Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)** for physical access.
    - Even if an attacker successfully clones an RFID card (something you *have*), they would still need:
        - Something you *know* (e.g., a **PIN** entered on a keypad).
        - Something you *are* (e.g., **biometrics** like a fingerprint or facial scan).
    - This layered approach significantly mitigates the risk of RFID cloning.

### Environmental Attacks
- **Description**: Attacking the **operating environment** that supports the technology, rather than directly attacking the IT systems themselves. The goal is to disrupt the conditions necessary for systems to function.
- **Attack Strategy**: If direct access to systems is difficult, attackers may target the surrounding infrastructure.

#### Types of Environmental Attacks

1.  **Power Monitoring/Disruption**
    - **Nature**: An obvious but highly effective attack.
    - **Execution**: Can be performed **from outside the building** (e.g., cutting power lines, tampering with external power infrastructure).
    - **Requirement**: Attackers do not need special access inside the facility.
    - **Impact**: Causes immediate system shutdown, leading to a [[Denial of Service (DoS)]].

2.  **HVAC (Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning) and Humidity Controls**
    - **Vulnerability**: HVAC systems often receive **lower security priority** compared to IT infrastructure, making them a potential weak point.
    - **Importance**: Large data centers and server rooms must be properly cooled and maintain specific humidity levels to prevent equipment failure.
    - **Attack Scenario**: An attacker gains access to HVAC control systems.
    - **Action**: Turn off cooling or manipulate humidity.
    - **Impact**:
        - **Overheating**: Systems heat up rapidly, leading to automatic shutdown to prevent damage, resulting in [[Denial of Service (DoS)]].
        - **Humidity**: High humidity can cause condensation and electrical shorts; low humidity can lead to static discharge, both damaging equipment.

3.  **Fire Suppression Systems**
    - **Vulnerability**: Gaining control or triggering these systems can cause significant disruption.
    - **Attack Scenario**: An attacker gains access to and triggers the fire suppression system.
    - **Impact**:
        - Can cause a cascade of events leading to a [[Denial of Service (DoS)]].
        - Some systems (e.g., gas-based suppression) can damage sensitive electronic equipment, even if no fire is present.
        - Evacuation protocols triggered by false alarms can also disrupt operations.

## Takeaways for Exam Preparation

1.  **Physical Access is the Ultimate Bypass**: Remember the fundamental principle: physical access to a server or device grants full control, bypassing digital security. This makes physical security non-negotiable.
2.  **Layers of Defense Apply to Physical Security**: Just like digital security, physical security requires a defense-in-depth strategy. Door locks alone are insufficient.
3.  **Brute Force (Physical) is a Real Threat**: Don't dismiss simple physical attacks. Evaluate your physical perimeter (doors, windows, walls) for weaknesses that could be exploited by force.
4.  **RFID Cloning is Easy and Cheap**:
    - Understand how RFID cloning works (read/copy in seconds).
    - Know that cheap tools are readily available for attackers.
    - **Crucially, remember that Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) is the primary defense against RFID cloning.** An access card (something you *have*) must be combined with a PIN (something you *know*) or biometrics (something you *are*) to be secure.
5.  **Environmental Attacks Target the *Support Infrastructure***:
    - These are indirect attacks aimed at disrupting the *conditions* for technology operation.
    - **Power disruption**: An obvious, often external, [[Denial of Service (DoS)]] vector.
    - **HVAC manipulation**: A critical vulnerability, as data centers rely heavily on cooling. Compromising HVAC can lead to overheating and system shutdowns (DoS). Note the potential for lower security priority on these systems.
    - **Fire suppression activation**: Can cause immediate DoS and potentially equipment damage.
6.  **Security Audits Must Include Physical Aspects**: Regular assessments of physical security controls (locks, cameras, access points, environmental controls) are just as important as network vulnerability scans.
7.  **Think Like an Attacker**: When designing security, consider how a determined individual might try to gain physical access or disrupt the environment.
