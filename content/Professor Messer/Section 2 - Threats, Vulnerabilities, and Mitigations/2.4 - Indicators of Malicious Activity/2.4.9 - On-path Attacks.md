# On-path Attacks

## Overview
An **on-path attack**, also known as a man-in-the-middle (MITM) attack, allows an attacker to intercept and potentially modify traffic between two communicating devices without either party knowing. The attacker effectively redirects the victim's traffic through their own device.

### 1. On-path Network Attack (General)
- **Definition**: An attacker positions themselves logically between two communicating devices.
    - This enables the attacker to observe all traffic flowing between these systems.
- **Mechanism**:
    - **Redirects Traffic**: The attacker's goal is to make the legitimate devices believe the attacker is the intended recipient or sender.
    - **Passes Traffic On**: After intercepting, the attacker forwards the traffic to the actual destination to maintain the appearance of a normal connection.
    - **Invisibility**: Victims are unaware their traffic has been redirected or intercepted. This makes the attack very difficult to detect from the victim's perspective.
- **Capabilities of Attacker**:
    - **Watch/Eavesdrop**: See all information being sent.
    - **Modify**: Change information in real-time as it traverses the network.
    - **Deny Service**: Drop traffic, effectively severing the connection.

### 2. ARP Poisoning (On-path Attack Example)
- **Definition**: A specific type of on-path attack that occurs on a local IP subnet.
- **Prerequisite**: The attacker must be on the same local subnet as the victim devices.
- **Vulnerability**: The Address Resolution Protocol (ARP) has no inherent security or encryption, making it easy to exploit.

#### How ARP Works Normally
1. **Need for MAC Address**: Devices communicate using MAC addresses at the data link layer. If a device needs to resolve an IP address to its MAC address, it sends an ARP request.
2. **ARP Request**: The device broadcasts: "Who has 192.168.1.1? Tell me your MAC address."
3. **ARP Reply**: The device with that IP responds with: "I am 192.168.1.1, and my MAC is `00:09:5b:d4:bb:fe`."
4. **ARP Cache Update**: The requesting device stores the IP-to-MAC mapping in its ARP cache.

#### How ARP Poisoning Works
1. **Attacker's Goal**: Make both devices think the attacker’s MAC address belongs to each other.
2. **Attacker Sends Unsolicited ARP Reply**:
    - To the laptop: "I am 192.168.1.1 (the router's IP), and my MAC is `aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff`."
        - The laptop updates its ARP cache to include `192.168.1.1 = aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff`.
    - To the router: "I am 192.168.1.9 (the laptop's IP), and my MAC is `aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff`."
        - The router updates its ARP cache to include `192.168.1.9 = aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff`.
3. **Traffic Redirection**:
    - When the laptop sends traffic to 192.168.1.1, it routes it to `aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff` (the attacker).
    - When the router sends traffic to 192.168.1.9, it routes it to `aa:bb:cc:dd:ee:ff` (the attacker).

### 3. On-path Browser Attack
- **Definition**: An on-path attack where the "middleman" is running malware on the same computer as the victim.
    - Formerly known as a man-in-the-browser (MITB) attack.

#### Mechanism:
1. **Malware/Trojan**: The attacker uses malware to intercept and manipulate browser traffic.
2. **Proxy Functionality**: The malware acts as a local proxy, intercepting and modifying traffic before it leaves the victim's machine or is displayed to the user.

#### Advantages for Attackers:
- **Encrypted Traffic**: Can easily bypass encrypted network traffic (HTTPS) because the malware can intercept data after decryption by the browser.
- **Normal Appearance**: Everything appears normal to the victim, as security indicators like the padlock remain valid despite the attacker's actions.

#### Attack Flow
1. **Malware Installs and Waits** for the victim to log into sensitive sites (e.g., online banking).
2. **Captures Information**: The malware captures all information entered by the victim.
3. **Hidden Sessions**: Initiates hidden sessions in the background, making unauthorized transactions without the victim's knowledge.

---

## Takeaways

1. **Define On-path Attack / Man-in-the-Middle (MITM)**: An attack where an adversary secretly relays and possibly alters communication between two parties who believe they are directly communicating with each other.
2. **Core Capabilities of On-path Attacks**: Eavesdropping, data modification, and denial of service.
3. **ARP Poisoning**:
    - **Type**: Network-level on-path attack.
    - **Scope**: Local IP subnet.
    - **Vulnerability Exploited**: ARP's lack of authentication/security.
    - **Mechanism**: The attacker sends fake ARP replies to map their MAC address to the IP addresses of legitimate devices, tricking both into sending traffic through the attacker.
    - **Attacker Location**: Must be on the same local network segment.

4. **On-path Browser Attack / Man-in-the-Browser (MITB)**:
    - **Type**: Application-level on-path attack.
    - **Scope**: Occurs on the victim's own computer.
    - **Mechanism**: Malware installed on the victim’s machine acts as a local proxy, intercepting and manipulating browser traffic.
    - **Key Advantages for Attacker**: Can easily bypass encrypted network traffic (HTTPS).
    - **Impacts**: Credential theft, hidden transactions, session hijacking.
    - **Detection Difficulty**: Extremely hard to detect by the victim.
