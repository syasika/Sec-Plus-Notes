# 1. Introduction to Phishing

- **Definition**: A form of social engineering with a touch of spoofing.
- **Delivery Methods**: Often delivered via email, text message (SMS), or other communication methods.
- **Goal**: To deceive individuals into believing something is legitimate when it is not, thereby tricking them into revealing private information.
- **Effectiveness**: Can be remarkably successful when well executed.
- **Information Sought**:
    - Usernames and passwords for various services.
    - Private personal information.

## 2. Identifying Phishing Attempts

### "Don't Be Fooled" Principles
- **Check the URL**: Legitimate links point to trusted sites. Phishing links often direct to different, suspicious locations. *Always hover over links to see the actual URL before clicking.*
- **Look for Inconsistencies**:
    - Spelling errors.
    - Incorrect fonts or graphics.
    - Poor formatting or layout.
    
### Example
A fake Rackspace login page shown with spacing and font issues, and missing elements compared to the real page.

## 3. Business Email Compromise (BEC)

- **Core Principle**: Attackers exploit the inherent trust people have in email sources.
- **Spoofed Email Addresses**:
    - Attackers use email addresses that are not legitimate but appear to be from a trusted source.
    - Can be a slight misspelling of a legitimate domain (e.g., `professormessor.com` instead of `professormesser.com`).
- **Impact of Compromised Email**:
    - Attackers can send emails from your account.
    - Attackers can read existing emails for sensitive information.
    - Can be used to reset passwords on other services (e.g., PayPal), as password reset links are often sent to the email account.
    - Clicking malicious links in these emails can lead to malware infection.

## 4. Tricks and Misdirection Techniques

### How Attackers Succeed
Through "digital sleight of hand" and manipulation.

### Typosquatting

- **Type of URL Hijacking**: Attackers register domain names that are slight variations or common misspellings of legitimate websites.
- **Example**: `professormessor.com` (note the 'o') instead of `professormesser.com`.

### Pretexting

- Involves lying or creating a fabricated scenario to trick someone into divulging information.
- The attacker assumes a character in a situation they create.
- *Example*: A phone call from "Visa" about a failed automated utility payment, asking for credit card details. The story creates urgency and a reason to give up information.

## 5. Phishing with Different Bait: Variations

### Vishing (Voice Phishing)

- **Phishing Conducted Over the Phone or via Voicemail**.
- **Caller ID Spoofing**: Common technique where the attacker manipulates the caller ID to display a legitimate-looking number (e.g., your bank's number).
- **Tactics**: Fake security checks, urgent bank updates, etc., to pressure victims into giving up account details or login credentials.

### Smishing (SMS Phishing)

- **Phishing Conducted via Text Message**.
- **Spoofing**: Also a common problem, making the text appear from a legitimate source.
- **Tactics**: Forwarding malicious links or directly asking for personal information.
- *Example*: A text message from "USPS" claiming a package delivery is suspended due to an incorrect address, with a link to "correct" it. Clicking this link could lead to a fake login page or malware.

### Other Variations

- **Fake Check Scam**.
- **Phone Verification Code Scam**.
- **Boss/CEO Scam (Impersonating a Superior for Urgent Financial Requests)**.
- **Advance-Fee Scam (e.g., Nigerian Prince Scam)**.

## Exam Takeaways

Here are the key points you should be able to define, identify, and explain for an exam:

1. **Phishing Definition**: Understand that phishing is a social engineering attack using spoofing, typically via email or text, to trick victims into revealing sensitive information.
2. **Common Phishing Indicators**:
    - **Suspicious URLs**: Know to hover over links to check the actual destination, which will likely be different from the displayed text.
    - **Grammar/Spelling Errors**: These are classic signs of unprofessional, illegitimate messages.
    - **Unusual Formatting/Graphics**: Inconsistencies or low-quality visuals compared to legitimate communications.
    - **Sense of Urgency/Threats**: Phishing attempts often create panic (e.g., account blocked, package suspended) to bypass critical thinking.
    - **Generic Greetings**: "Dear User" instead of your name.
    - **Incorrect Sender Details**: The "from" address might be subtly wrong or from an unrelated domain (e.g., `@icloud.com` for a "Rackspace" email).
3. **Business Email Compromise (BEC)**: Recognize this as a specific type of phishing where attackers leverage trust in email to gain access to accounts or sensitive data. Be aware of how spoofed email addresses are used here.
4. **Key Misdirection Techniques**:
    - **Typosquatting**: Understand it as URL hijacking using misspelled or similar domain names.
    - **Pretexting**: Define this as creating a believable (but false) story or scenario to manipulate a victim into providing information.
5. **Phishing Variations**:
    - **Vishing (Voice Phishing)**: Phishing via phone calls or voicemail, often using caller ID spoofing.
    - **Smishing (SMS Phishing)**: Phishing via text messages, also prone to spoofing, typically includes malicious links or requests for personal data.
6. **Impact of Successful Phishing**:
    - Compromised login credentials.
    - Financial fraud (e.g., unauthorized transactions, wire transfer modifications).
    - Identity theft.
    - Malware infection.
7. **Best Practices for Prevention**:
    - Never click on suspicious links in emails or text messages.
    - Always verify the sender and the legitimacy of the request through an independent channel (e.g., calling the official company number, navigating directly to their website).
    - Be skeptical of unsolicited communications, especially those demanding urgent action or sensitive information.
    - Check for subtle discrepancies in domain names and sender addresses.
    - Be aware of common scam types to recognize patterns.
