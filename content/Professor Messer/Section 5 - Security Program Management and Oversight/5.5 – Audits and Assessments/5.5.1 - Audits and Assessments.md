# Audits and Assessments

## Introduction to Audits
- The term "audit" often carries a negative connotation, such as tax audits. However, auditing your technology, especially in the context of IT security, can be very beneficial.

## Cybersecurity Audit
- **Definition:** A thorough examination of various aspects of a computing environment.
- **What it examines:**
    - IT infrastructure (servers, networks, etc.)
    - Software (applications, operating systems, custom code)
    - Devices (endpoints, mobile devices, IoT devices)
    - **Policies and procedures** to ensure they are effective and followed.
- **Key Objectives/Benefits:**
    - Checks for the effectiveness of existing policies and procedures.
    - Helps find vulnerabilities before attackers can exploit them.
    - Ultimately, makes the organization much safer.
- **Execution:**
    - Can be performed internally by the organization's own staff.
    - Can be performed by an independent third-party for a thorough and unbiased overview.

## Attestation
- **Definition:** Provides an opinion of truth or accuracy regarding a company's security positioning.
- **Relationship to Audit:** An auditor will first perform an audit, and then they will attest to the company's cybersecurity posture based on the findings of that audit.
- *Think of it as the formal statement or certification of the audit's results.*

## Internal Audits
- Audits performed within the organization, not necessarily by external entities.
- **Purpose:**
    - **Compliance:** To determine if the organization is complying with relevant regulatory or industry requirements (e.g., HIPAA, GDPR, PCI DSS, ISO 27001).
- **Key Components:**
    - **Audit Committee:**
        - **Role:** Oversees all risk management activities within the organization.
        - **Responsibility:** All internal audits start and stop with this committee (they initiate, guide, and review the audits).
    - **Self-assessments:**
        - The organization performs its own checks against established standards, policies, or requirements.
        - These self-assessments are then consolidated into ongoing reports to provide an overall picture of the organization's compliance and security posture.
        - *Benefit: Allows proactive identification of gaps and continuous monitoring of internal controls.*

## External Audits
- Audits performed by an independent third-party.
- **Primary Driver:** Often necessitated by regulatory requirements.
    - An independent third-party may be required to perform the audit by law or industry standard.
    - The audit type and frequency are typically dictated by the specific regulation.
- **Process/Components:**
    - **Examinations:**
        - Involves hands-on research by the external auditor.
        - Activities include viewing records and documentation, compiling reports based on findings, and gathering additional details through interviews, system inspections, etc.
    - **Assessment:**
        - The audit will assess current activities and the organization's adherence to requirements.
        - Provides recommendations for future improvements, highlighting areas where the company can enhance its security posture and compliance.
        - *The external auditor offers an objective view and actionable advice for remediation and enhancement.*

# Takeaways for Exam

## Differentiate Audits & Attestation
- **Audit:** The process of examining and verifying systems, policies, and procedures. It's the "doing" of the check.
- **Attestation:** The outcome or formal statement/opinion provided by an auditor about the truthfulness or accuracy of the security posture, based on the audit findings. It's the "reporting" of the check's results.
- **Key Point:** An audit leads to an attestation. You audit to get an attestation.

## Understand Cybersecurity Audit Purpose
- **Core Purpose:** To proactively identify weaknesses (vulnerabilities) in IT infrastructure, software, devices, and policies before malicious actors can exploit them.
- **Beyond Vulnerabilities:** Also checks if existing security policies and procedures are effective and actually being followed.
- **Overall Goal:** Enhance the organization's security and resilience against threats.

## Distinguish Internal vs. External Audits
- **Internal Audits:**
    - **Who:** Performed by the organization's own staff or departments.
    - **Why:** Primarily for internal compliance checks and self-improvement against regulations/standards.
    - **Key Players:** The Audit Committee initiates, oversees, and concludes internal audits.
    - **Method:** Often starts with self-assessments that are then consolidated.
    - **Benefit:** Continuous monitoring, early detection of issues, fostering a security-aware culture.
- **External Audits:**
    - **Who:** Performed by an independent third-party.
    - **Why:** Often mandated by regulatory requirements (e.g., laws like HIPAA, industry standards like PCI DSS) to provide an unbiased verification.
    - **Process:** Involves hands-on examinations (reviewing records, interviews, system checks) by the external auditor.
    - **Outcome:** An assessment of current activities and recommendations for improvements.
    - **Benefit:** Provides objective validation, builds trust with stakeholders (customers, regulators), ensures compliance with external obligations.

## Role of the Audit Committee
- Crucial for internal audits.
- **Oversees all risk management activities** within the organization.
- **Starts and stops all internal audits**, acting as the central governing body for these processes.

## Components of External Audits
- **Examinations:** The active, hands-on phase where the auditor collects data (records, reports, interviews).
- **Assessment:** The concluding phase where the auditor evaluates the collected data, states the current security posture, and provides actionable recommendations for improvement. This is where the value add for future security enhancements comes from.

## Regulatory Influence
- For external audits, the regulation itself dictates the type and frequency of the audit. Understanding the specific compliance landscape your organization operates within highlights its importance.
