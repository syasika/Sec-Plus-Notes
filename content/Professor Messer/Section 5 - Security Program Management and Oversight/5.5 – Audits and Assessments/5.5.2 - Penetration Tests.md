# Penetration Tests

## Key Terms
- **Penetration Testing (Pentesting):** A simulated cyberattack against your computer system to check for exploitable vulnerabilities.
- **Physical Penetration Testing:** Testing an organization's physical security measures to identify weaknesses that could lead to unauthorized access.
- **Red Team:** The offensive security team that simulates attacks.
- **Blue Team:** The defensive security team that defends against attacks and identifies them.
- **Integrated Team (Purple Team):** A collaborative approach where red and blue teams work together to improve security.
- **Working Knowledge:** The amount of information provided to the penetration testing team about the target environment.
- **Known Environment (White Box Test):** Full disclosure of the target system's information.
- **Partially Known Environment (Gray Box Test):** A mix of known and unknown information about the target system.
- **Unknown Environment (Black Box Test / Blind Test):** No prior information provided to the pentester, requiring them to discover everything.
- **Reconnaissance:** The initial phase of gathering information about a target before an attack.
- **Passive Reconnaissance:** Gathering information from open sources without directly interacting with the target's systems.
- **Active Reconnaissance:** Gathering information by directly interacting with the target's systems, which can be detectable.
- **Digital Footprint:** The trail of data left by a person or organization's online activity.

## Physical Penetration Testing

-   **Concept:** While often thought of as digital, physical pentesting is crucial for identifying real-world security gaps.
-   **Circumventing OS Security:**
    -   Operating system security can be bypassed through physical access.
    -   Methods:
        -   Modify the boot process.
        -   Boot from other media (e.g., USB drive with a different OS).
        -   Modify or replace OS files.
-   **Importance of Physical Security:**
    -   Physical security is key to preventing unauthorized individuals from gaining access.
    -   Servers are often locked in secure data centers due to this importance.
-   **Assessment Focus:**
    -   Can an unauthorized individual enter the building without a key?
    -   What access is available once inside?
    -   Testing includes: Doors, windows, elevators, and physical security processes (e.g., tailgating, social engineering).

## Pentesting Perspectives

-   **Offensive (Red Team):**
    -   **Role:** Attacks systems and looks for vulnerabilities to exploit.
    -   **Goal:** Simulate a real attacker to find weaknesses.
-   **Defensive (Blue Team):**
    -   **Role:** Identifies attacks in real-time and prevents unauthorized access.
    -   **Goal:** Protect the systems and respond to incidents.
-   **Integrated (Purple Team):**
    -   **Process:** Create an ongoing process where Red and Blue teams collaborate.
    -   **Workflow:** Red team identifies an opening/vulnerability -> shares with Blue team -> Blue team patches and improves detection/prevention -> Red team tests again.
    -   **Benefit:** Provides constant feedback and continuous improvement of security posture.

## Working Knowledge (Levels of Disclosure)

-   **Concept:** The amount of information given to the penetration testing team before the test. This dictates the approach.
-   **Known Environment (White Box Test):**
    -   **Disclosure:** Full disclosure of all systems, network diagrams, IP addresses, source code, etc.
    -   **Benefit:** Allows the pentester to focus on specific vulnerabilities and configurations quickly.
-   **Partially Known Environment (Gray Box Test):**
    -   **Disclosure:** A mix of known and unknown information.
    -   **Focus:** Often used to focus on certain systems or applications while others remain "unknown."
    -   **Analogy:** Simulates an insider threat or an attacker who has gained some initial access.
-   **Unknown Environment (Black Box Test / Blind Test):**
    -   **Disclosure:** The pentester knows nothing about the systems under attack.
    -   **Method:** They must find all information on their own, mimicking a real-world external attacker.
    -   **Benefit:** Provides the most realistic assessment of an organization's external security posture.

## Reconnaissance

-   **Concept:** The crucial initial phase of gathering information before launching any attack. "Can't rush blindly into battle."
-   **Purpose:**
    -   Gather a digital footprint: Learn everything possible about the target.
    -   Understand the security posture: Identify firewalls, security configurations, servers, applications.
    -   Minimize the attack area: Focus efforts on key systems that are most vulnerable or valuable.
    -   Create a network map: Identify routers, networks, remote sites, and their connections.

### Passive Reconnaissance

-   **Method:** Learning as much as possible from open sources without direct interaction with the target's systems.
-   **Detectability:** Difficult for the target to protect against or identify, as no direct interaction occurs.
-   **Examples:**
    -   Social media (LinkedIn, Twitter, Facebook for employee information, organizational announcements).
    -   Corporate website (publicly available information, job postings, technologies mentioned).
    -   Online forums, Reddit (discussions about the company, employee complaints, technical issues).
    -   Social engineering (manipulating people to divulge information, without direct system interaction).
    -   Dumpster diving (finding discarded documents with sensitive information).
    -   Business organizations/partners (information shared publicly or semi-publicly by partners).
    -   WHOIS lookups, DNS records, public IP address ranges.

### Active Reconnaissance

-   **Method:** Directly interacting with the target's systems to gather information.
-   **Detectability:** Visible on network traffic and logs; relatively easy to be seen.
-   **Examples:**
    -   **Ping scans:** Checking if hosts are alive.
    -   **Port scans:** Identifying open ports and services running on target systems.
    -   **DNS queries:** Direct queries to corporate DNS servers to map internal infrastructure.
    -   **OS scans / OS fingerprinting:** Determining the operating system of target devices.
    -   **Service scans / Version scans:** Identifying specific services and their versions running on open ports (e.g., HTTP server version, SSH version).

## Takeaways for Exam Success

1.  **Physical vs. Digital Pentesting:** Don't forget physical security! Understand *why* it's important (circumventing OS security) and *how* it's tested (doors, windows, social engineering).
2.  **Team Roles:** Clearly distinguish between Red (offensive), Blue (defensive), and the benefit of an Integrated/Purple Team (continuous improvement).
3.  **Working Knowledge is Key:** Memorize the three levels of disclosure (Known, Partially Known, Unknown) and their corresponding industry terms (White Box, Gray Box, Black Box/Blind Test). Understand what information is available in each scenario.
4.  **Reconnaissance is Foundational:** Recognize that information gathering is the first critical step in any pentest. You can't attack effectively without knowing your target.
5.  **Passive vs. Active Reconnaissance:** This is a crucial distinction.
    -   **Passive:** Learn from *open sources*, *no direct interaction*, *hard to detect*. Think OSINT (Open Source Intelligence).
    -   **Active:** Learn via *direct interaction* with systems, *detectable* (leaves logs, network traffic), provides *more specific* technical data.
6.  **Examples Matter:** Be able to list specific examples for both passive (social media, corporate website, dumpster diving) and active reconnaissance (ping scans, port scans, DNS queries, OS fingerprinting, service/version scans). These are common exam questions.
7.  **Why Reconnaissance?** It helps to understand the target's security posture, narrow down the attack surface, and build a network map.
