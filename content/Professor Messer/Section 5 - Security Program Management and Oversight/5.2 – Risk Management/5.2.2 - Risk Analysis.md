# Risk Analysis - Professor Messer Notes

## 1. Qualitative Risk Assessment
- **Definition:** Evaluates risk factors using broad, subjective terms (e.g., low, medium, high).
- **Purpose:** To identify significant risk factors and get a high-level view to focus efforts.
- **Methodology:**
    - Identify key risk factors such as legacy Windows clients, untrained staff, or no anti-virus software.
    - Gather expert opinions on the significance of impact, occurrence, and cost of controls.
    - Visualize results using a traffic light grid (red for high, yellow for medium, green for low).
- **Example Walkthrough:**
    - **Legacy Windows Clients:**
        - Impact: Medium (Yellow)
        - Annualized Rate of Occurrence (ARO): High (Red) - Many clients need updates.
        - Cost of Controls: Medium (Yellow)
        - Overall Risk: High (Red)
    - **Untrained Staff:**
        - Impact: Low (Green)
        - ARO: Medium (Yellow)
        - Cost of Controls: Low (Green)
        - Overall Risk: Medium (Yellow)
    - **No Anti-Virus Software:**
        - Impact: Medium (Yellow)
        - ARO: High (Red)
        - Cost of Controls: Medium (Yellow)
        - Overall Risk: Very High (Red)
- **Key Characteristics:** Subjective, opinion-based, provides a general understanding.

## 2. Quantitative Risk Assessment
- **Definition:** Calculates specific monetary values for risks to allow precise financial planning.
- **Key Metrics & Formulas:**
    - **ARO (Annualized Rate of Occurrence):** How likely is it that a risk event will occur in a single year?
        - Example: Hurricane hitting Montana (low ARO) vs. Florida (high ARO).
    - **Asset Value (AV):** The total value of the asset to the organization, including cost, sales impact, regulatory fines, legal costs, lost productivity, and goodwill damage.
    - **Exposure Factor (EF):** The percentage of the asset's value lost due to a single incident, expressed as a decimal (e.g., 0.25 for losing a quarter, 1.0 for losing the entire asset).
    - **SLE (Single Loss Expectancy):** The monetary loss if a single risk event occurs.
        - Formula: `SLE = Asset Value (AV) x Exposure Factor (EF)`
        - Example: Laptop stolen = $1,000 (AV) x 1.0 (EF) = $1,000 (SLE)
    - **ALE (Annualized Loss Expectancy):** The total monetary loss expected from a risk over a year.
        - Formula: `ALE = Annualized Rate of Occurrence (ARO) x Single Loss Expectancy (SLE)`
        - Example: Seven laptops stolen a year (ARO=7) x $1,000 (SLE) = $7,000 (ALE)
- **Key Characteristics:** Objective, data-driven, focuses on financial impact.
- **Business Impact:** Can extend beyond monetary losses to include reputation and legal issues.

## 3. Impact Categories
- Consider various types of impacts when assessing risks:
    - **Life:** Most important; cannot replace people.
    - **Property:** Risk to buildings, physical assets, equipment.
    - **Safety:** Risk to individuals' well-being in dangerous environments.
    - **Finance:** Monetary cost (covered by quantitative analysis).
    - **Reputation/Goodwill:** Damage to public image and customer trust.
    - **Legal/Regulatory:** Fines and compliance violations.

## 4. Likelihood and Probability
- **Risk Likelihood:** A qualitative measurement of risk, examples: rare, possible, almost certain.
- **Risk Probability:** A quantitative measurement based on historical performance or future expectations.
- **Relationship:** Often similar in scope but can be used interchangeably in casual conversation. Can be converted (e.g., calculate probability, then assign a likelihood category).

## 5. Risk Appetite and Tolerance
- **Risk Appetite:**
    - A broad description of the acceptable risk level an organization is willing to take.
    - The amount of risk before taking action to reduce it.
- **Risk Appetite Posture:** Qualitative description of the organization's readiness to take risks, e.g., conservative, neutral, or expansionary.
- **Risk Tolerance:**
    - An upper limit for acceptable risk, beyond which action must be taken.
- **Analogy (Speed Limit):** 
    - **Risk Appetite (Speed Limit 55 mph):** Government sets the official "acceptable" balance between safety and convenience.
    - **Risk Tolerance (Ticketing threshold):** Action is taken only if the speed exceeds a certain amount, e.g., 65 mph.

## 6. Risk Register
- **Definition:** A centralized document that identifies and documents risks associated with each step of a project or operation.
- **Purpose:**
    - To track and manage risks throughout their lifecycle.
    - To ensure everyone understands the risks and proposed solutions.
- **Components (per entry):**
    - **ID:** Unique identifier.
    - **Date Raised:** When the risk was identified.
    - **Risk Description:** Clear, concise explanation of the risk.
    - **Likelihood of Risk Occurring:** Qualitative (e.g., Low, Medium, High).
    - **Impact if Risk Occurs:** Qualitative (e.g., Low, Medium, High).
    - **Severity:** Overall risk rating using colors like green, yellow, red to represent likelihood and impact.
    - **Owner:** Person responsible for managing/monitoring the risk.
    - **Mitigating Action:** Steps to reduce the likelihood or impact of the risk.
    - **Contingent Action:** Fallback plan if the risk happens.
    - **Progress on Actions:** Status updates on mitigation/contingency efforts.
    - **Status:** Current state of the risk (e.g., Open, Closed).
- **Key Risk Indicators (KRIs):**
    - Metrics or signs that identify risks impacting the organization.
    - Each line in the risk register represents a KRI.
    - Example KRIs: Undefined project purpose, incomplete design, unclear schedule.
- **Risk Owners:** Assigned to individuals responsible for managing specific risks.
- **Risk Threshold:** The point at which the cost of mitigation equals the value gained by it. Helps balance investment with potential benefits.

## Takeaways for Exam Preparation:
1. **Understand Qualitative vs. Quantitative Risk Assessment:**
    - **Qualitative:** Subjective, uses broad terms (low/medium/high), good for initial screening and communication.
        - Think "traffic light grid."
    - **Quantitative:** Objective, specific monetary values using formulas, good for financial justification and detailed analysis.
2. **Memorize Quantitative Risk Assessment Formulas:**
    - `SLE = AV x EF`
    - `ALE = ARO x SLE`
    - Be able to define ARO, AV (including hidden costs), EF, SLE, and ALE.
3. **Know Key Impact Categories Beyond Financial Costs:**
    - Life (most important), Property, Safety, Finance, Reputation, Legal/Regulatory.
4. **Differentiate Between Risk Likelihood and Probability:**
    - Likelihood is qualitative (e.g., "possible"), while probability is quantitative (e.g., "20% chance").
5. **Clearly Distinguish Between Risk Appetite and Tolerance:**
    - **Appetite:** Desired level of risk an organization accepts.
    - **Tolerance:** Maximum acceptable deviation before intervention, e.g., how much over the speed limit you can go before getting a ticket.
6. **Understand the Purpose and Key Elements of a Risk Register:**
    - It’s a project management tool for documenting, tracking, and managing risks.
    - Be able to identify and explain terms like KRIs, risk owners, mitigating actions, contingent actions, and risk threshold.
7. **Focus on the "Why" Behind Each Concept:**
    - Understand why we use qualitative vs. quantitative assessments.
    - Why certain impacts are prioritized.
    - The importance of risk appetite and tolerance in decision-making.
