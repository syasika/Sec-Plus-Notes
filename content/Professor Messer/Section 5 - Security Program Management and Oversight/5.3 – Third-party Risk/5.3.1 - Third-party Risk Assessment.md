# Third-Party Risk Assessment - Professor Messer Notes

## 1. Third-Party Risk

### Definition
Third-party risk refers to the risks introduced when an organization works with external vendors for various services or raw materials.

### Examples of Vendors
- Payroll services
- Customer relationship management (CRM)
- Email marketing
- Travel management
- Raw material suppliers

### Data Sharing
Important company data is often shared with third parties, especially for cloud-based services. For example, a payroll company might receive sensitive employee data.

### Mitigation Strategy
To mitigate these risks:

1. **Perform a Risk Assessment:** Categorize risk by vendor and manage it accordingly.
2. **Use Contracts for Clear Understanding:** Ensure everyone understands expectations and can enforce a secure environment, with penalties for breach of agreement.

## 2. Penetration Testing (Pentest)

### Definition
Penetration testing (pentesting) simulates attacks on a system or network to find vulnerabilities and exploit them.

#### Distinction from Vulnerability Scanning
- **Vulnerability Scanning:** Identifies vulnerabilities but does not actively try to exploit them.
- **Pentesting:** Actively tries to exploit identified vulnerabilities to test the system's robustness.

### Compliance Mandate
Often required by internal policies or legal agreements, such as regular pentests (e.g., quarterly or annually).

### Third-Party Involvement
Often performed by specialized third-party companies for an unbiased assessment and expert insight into the latest attack techniques. They provide reports for both the organization and the vendor on security posture.

## 3. Rules of Engagement (ROE)

### Purpose
A crucial document defining the purpose, scope, and parameters of a penetration test to make everyone aware of what will be tested and how.

### Key Parameters

1. **Type of Testing:**
   - On-site physical breach (e.g., social engineering, physical access attempts)
   - Internal network test
   - External network test (across the internet)

2. **Schedule:**
   - Specific dates and times
   - Normal working hours, after-hours, etc.

3. **Specific Rules:**
   - **IP address ranges:** Which systems/networks are included.
   - **Emergency contacts:** Who to notify if something goes wrong.
   - **Handling sensitive information:** Procedures for data encountered during the test.
   - **In-scope/Out-of-scope devices/applications:** Clearly defines what can and cannot be touched.

## 4. Right-to-Audit Clauses

### Common Scenarios
Essential when working with business partners, especially for:

1. **Data Sharing:** When sensitive data is entrusted to a third party (e.g., payroll).
2. **Outsourcing:** When critical functions are handled by a third party (e.g., IT infrastructure, cloud providers).

### Third-Party Providers
They might hold all your data or manage critical services like internet access. The core question: *Are they secure?*

### Contractual Requirement
Incorporate the "Right-to-Audit" clause into contracts to ensure the option to perform a security audit at any time.

#### Legal Agreement
Grants the organization the ability to verify security before and after engagement, ensuring mutual understanding of terms and conditions.

## 5. Evidence of Internal Audits

### Objective
Evaluate the effectiveness of security controls through third-party audits for objectivity.

### Compliance
May be required by specific regulations or industry standards. Good practice even without mandates.

### Focus Areas
Audits check for security controls and processes, including:

- Access management (who has access to what)
- Off-boarding procedures (revoking access for departing employees)
- Password security (policies, storage, complexity)
- VPN controls (access, configuration)

#### Opportunity for Improvement
Audits almost always reveal areas where security can be enhanced.

### Frequency
Should be performed at a reasonable frequency (e.g., regularly scheduled audits) to ensure ongoing effectiveness.

## 6. Supply Chain Analysis

### Definition
The system involved in creating a product or service, from raw materials to the final customer, involving organizations, people, activities, and resources.

### Security Concerns
Security concerns exist at every step of the supply chain.

### Steps in Analysis

1. **Understand the Flow:** How a product/service flows from supplier to customer.
2. **Evaluate Coordination:** Assess coordination between different groups (internal and external).
3. **Identify Areas for Improvement:** Look for security and efficiency improvements.
4. **Assess IT Systems:** Evaluate the IT systems supporting the operation.
5. **Document Changes:** Record any business process changes.

### Real-World Example: SolarWinds Attack

#### Timeline
Malware installed in software updates between March-June 2020.

#### Announcement
December 2020 by SolarWinds.

#### Method
Attackers breached SolarWinds, injected malware into their product's code, and deployed it with a valid *SolarWinds digital signature*.

#### Impact
At least 18,000 of 300,000 customers potentially impacted (including government agencies and major corporations).

#### Lesson
Highlighted the critical importance of supply chain security and vetting vendors thoroughly.

## 7. Independent Assessments

### Role
An important part of both due diligence (pre-contract) and ongoing vendor monitoring (post-contract), providing direct answers from the vendor on security practices and plans.

### Security-Related Questions
- What is the vendor's due diligence process?
- What disaster recovery plans are in place?
- What secure storage methods are used for company data?
- How is data protected?

#### Updating Risk Analysis
The results from questionnaires update the vendor’s risk analysis, which should be continually updated throughout the life of the vendor relationship.

## 8. Vendor Selection Process

### Due Diligence
Thoroughly check a company before doing business with them.

1. **Investigation and Verification:** Investigate and verify information provided by the vendor.
2. **Key Checks:**
   - Financial status (stability)
   - Pending or past legal issues
   - Background checks on key personnel
   - Personnel interviews

### Conflict of Interest
Situations where personal interests could compromise professional judgment.

#### Examples
- A potential partner also does business with your largest competitor.
- A third-party employs a relative of your CFO (Chief Financial Officer).
- A third-party offers gifts if a contract is signed (potential for bribery/undue influence).

### Impact
Conflicts of interest can prevent business relationships due to ethical or legal concerns.

## 9. Vendor Monitoring

### Ongoing Management
The vendor relationship doesn’t end when the contract is signed; continuous monitoring is essential.

1. **Regular Reviews:** Financial health checks, IT security reviews.
2. **Monitor News and Social Media:** Keep an eye on any issues related to the vendor.

### Tailored Monitoring
Different vendors may require different indicators for monitoring (quantitative and qualitative analysis).

### Assigned Responsibility
Assign a specific person or team to manage the vendor relationship and monitor it continuously.

## 10. Questionnaires

### Role
An important part of due diligence (pre-contract) and ongoing vendor monitoring (post-contract), allowing you to get direct answers from the vendor.

### Security-Related Questions
- What is the vendor's due diligence process?
- What disaster recovery plans are in place?
- What secure storage methods are used for company data?
- How is data protected?

#### Updating Risk Analysis
The results from questionnaires update the vendor’s risk analysis, which should be continually updated throughout the life of the vendor relationship.

## Key Takeaways for the Exam

1. **Third-Party Risk is Ubiquitous:** Every organization relies on vendors, and this reliance inherently introduces risk due to shared data and outsourced functions.
2. **Proactive Risk Assessment is Crucial:** Don't assume vendors are secure. Actively assess their security posture before and during the engagement.
3. **Contracts Are Your Legal Backstop:** Incorporate security requirements, risk assessments, and clauses like "Right-to-Audit" directly into your contracts to establish clear expectations and legal recourse.
4. **Penetration Testing (Pentesting):** Understand its definition (simulating attacks to exploit vulnerabilities) and its distinction from vulnerability scanning (identifying vulnerabilities). It's a critical tool for validating security.
5. **Rules of Engagement (ROE) Are Essential for Pentesting:** This document defines the scope, type, schedule, and specific rules for a pentest, ensuring all parties are aligned and minimizing unintended consequences.
6. **Right-to-Audit Clauses Empower You:** These contractual clauses give your organization the legal right to inspect a vendor’s security controls, providing assurance that your data and services are protected.
7. **Internal Audits and Third-Party Audits:** Regularly evaluate the effectiveness of security controls. Using third-party auditors provides an independent and expert perspective, often required for compliance.
8. **Supply Chain Security Is a Major Concern:** Recognize that security vulnerabilities can originate anywhere in the complex supply chain. The SolarWinds attack is a prime example of a supply chain compromise.
9. **Independent Assessments Offer Fresh Perspectives:** External experts can identify security gaps or suggest improvements that internal teams might overlook due to their focused perspective.
10. **Due Diligence and Conflict of Interest in Vendor Selection:** Thoroughly vet potential vendors (due diligence). Be vigilant for conflicts of interest that could compromise judgment or lead to unethical practices.
11. **Vendor Monitoring Is Ongoing, Not a One-Time Event:** The relationship with a vendor requires continuous oversight, including financial health checks, security reviews, and monitoring public information, adjusted for each vendor’s specific risks.
12. **Questionnaires Are Practical Tools:** Use questionnaires during due diligence and ongoing monitoring to gather direct information from vendors about their security practices and plans. The responses help update your risk analysis.
