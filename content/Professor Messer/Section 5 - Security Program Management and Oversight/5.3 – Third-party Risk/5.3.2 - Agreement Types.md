# Agreement Types

## Service Level Agreement (SLA)

- **Definition**: A minimum set of terms for services provided.
- **Purpose**: Ensures clarity on service expectations between parties.
- **Common Elements**:
  - **Uptime/Availability**: Guaranteed operational time, such as 99.99% uptime.
  - **Response Time**: Timeframe for the service provider to respond to issues.
  - **Resolution Time**: Expected time to resolve reported problems.
  - **Metrics**: Specific, measurable targets for service performance.
- **Usage**: Commonly used between customers and service providers (e.g., Internet Service Provider - ISP, cloud service provider).
- **Example Scenario (ISP Contract)**:
  - SLA states "no more than four hours of unscheduled downtime."
  - Requires a technician to be dispatched if downtime occurs.
  - May require the customer to keep spare equipment on-site for quicker resolution by the ISP.
- **Key Point**: Both parties understand the exact requirements for service levels. It's a legally binding contract.

## Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)

- **Definition**: An agreement where both sides agree in general to the contents of the memorandum.
- **Nature**:
  - Usually states common goals and intentions, but not much more.
  - **Informal letter of intent; not a signed contract.**
- **Purpose**: A broad overview of how two organizations might work together in the future. Often the first step in a potential partnership.

## Memorandum of Agreement (MOA)

- **Definition**: The next step above an MOU, showing a stronger commitment than an MOU.
- **Nature**:
  - Both sides conditionally agree to the objectives.
  - Can also be a legal document, even without legal language.
- **Purpose**: Describes the relationship between two organizations in more detail than an MOU, outlining specific actions or responsibilities, but often without the full legal weight of a contract.

## Master Service Agreement (MSA)

- **Definition**: A legal contract and agreement of terms that establishes a broad framework for an ongoing relationship.
- **Nature**:
  - A broad framework to cover later transactions.
  - Many detailed negotiations happen here (e.g., billing, payment terms, general responsibilities, dispute resolution).
  - Future projects will be based on this agreement.
- **Purpose**: To avoid renegotiating fundamental terms for every new project or service provided by the same vendor. It sets the overarching rules and conditions for all subsequent work.

## Work Order (WO) / Statement of Work (SOW)

- **Definition**: A specific list of items to be completed, detailing the specific tasks for a project.
- **Usage**: Used in conjunction with an MSA (Master Service Agreement).
- **Content**:
  - Details the scope of the job (what will be done).
  - Location where the work will take place.
  - Deliverables schedule (what will be produced and when).
  - Acceptance criteria (how success will be measured).
- **Purpose**: Provides granular detail for individual projects or tasks under the umbrella of a broader MSA. If there's a question about whether a job was done properly, refer to the SOW.
- **Relationship to MSA**: The MSA lays out the general terms of the relationship, while the SOW specifies the details of a particular job within that relationship, leveraging the terms established in the MSA without needing to redefine them.

## Non-disclosure Agreement (NDA)

- **Definition**: A confidentiality agreement between parties.
- **Purpose**: To ensure that information shared between parties will not be disclosed to unauthorized third parties.
- **Protects**:
  - Trade secrets.
  - Business activities.
  - Any other confidential information explicitly listed in the NDA.
- **Types**:
  - **Unilateral (one-way NDA)**: Only one party is obligated to maintain confidentiality.
  - **Bilateral (mutual NDA)**: Both parties are obligated to maintain confidentiality.
  - **Multilateral**: More than two parties are involved and all agree to confidentiality.
- **Nature**: A formal contract. Signatures are usually required before discussing the confidential terms. It is legally binding.

## Business Partners Agreement (BPA)

- **Definition**: An agreement established when individuals or entities are going into business together as formal partners.
- **Purpose**: To clearly define the terms of the partnership and prevent future disputes.
- **Key Elements**:
  - **Owner Stake**: Defines equity, ownership percentages, and contributions of each partner.
  - **Financial Contract**: Outlines financial responsibilities, profit/loss distribution, capital contributions, and withdrawal processes.
  - **Decision-making**: Specifies who makes business decisions, what decisions require unanimous consent, and the scope of individual authority.
  - **Contingencies**:
    - Financial issues: How to handle unexpected losses or cash flow problems.
    - Disaster recovery: How the business will continue or dissolve in case of unforeseen events (e.g., natural disaster, partner incapacitation).
    - Dispute resolution: Mechanisms for resolving disagreements between partners.
    - Exit strategies: How a partner can leave the business, or how the partnership can be dissolved.
- **Key Point**: A critical document for defining the operational, financial, and legal aspects of a business partnership.

## Key Takeaways for Exam Preparation:

1. **Hierarchy of Formality/Legality**:
   - **MOU** (Most informal, letter of intent, not binding)
   - **MOA** (More formal than MOU, conditional agreement, can be legal but not fully enforceable promises)
   - **SLA, MSA, NDA, BPA** (Formal, legally binding contracts with clear terms and enforceability)

2. **Purpose-Driven Differentiation**:
   - **SLA**: Defines service performance guarantees for external customers/providers.
   - **MOU/MOA**: Precursors to formal agreements, outlining intent and general objectives (MOU) or conditional agreement (MOA).
   - **MSA**: Establishes a long-term framework for ongoing work between parties.
   - **SOW/WO**: Details specific tasks/projects under an existing MSA.
   - **NDA**: Protects confidential information shared between parties.
   - **BPA**: Governs the internal workings and relationships of a business partnership.

3. **Key Distinctions to Memorize**:
   - MOU is not a contract; MOA can be legal but may not have enforceable promises. MSA is a legal contract.
   - MSA provides the framework, SOW provides the details for specific jobs.
   - NDA types: Unilateral (one-way), Bilateral (mutual), Multilateral.
   - BPA covers contingencies like financial issues, disaster recovery, and decision-making for partners.

4. **Application Scenarios**:
   - Hiring an ISP -> SLA
   - Exploring a new business venture -> MOU
   - Moving forward with a detailed collaboration plan (but not yet a full contract) -> MOA
   - Establishing a long-term vendor relationship -> MSA
   - Starting a specific project with that vendor -> SOW
   - Sharing sensitive company data with a potential partner -> NDA
   - Forming a company with co-founders -> BPA

5. **Legal Implications**: While some are informal, understand that SLA, MSA, NDA, and BPA are typically legally binding contracts, carrying consequences for non-compliance. MOUs and MOAs might have some legal weight, especially if they contain confidentiality clauses or clear conditional agreements, but their enforceability is generally weaker than a full contract.
