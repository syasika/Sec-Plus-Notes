# Security Procedures

## Change Management
### Definition
- A formal process for managing changes within an organization's systems.

### Purpose
- To avoid downtime, confusion, and mistakes.
- Ensure that nothing changes without following a predefined process.

### Steps in the Change Management Process:
1. **Determine the Scope of the Change:**
   - Is it a single server or multiple devices?
   - An entire OS update or a single file modification?

2. **Analyze the Risk Associated with the Change:**
   - What is the potential impact if something goes wrong?
   - Does it affect the entire OS or a specific application?

3. **Create a Plan:**
   - A detailed outline of what parts of the system will be changed and how.

4. **Get End-User Approval:**
   - Ensure affected users agree to the proposed changes.

5. **Present the Proposal to the Change Control Board (CCB):**
   - **CCB Role:** Analyzes all proposed changes, approves them, and schedules them.
   
6. **Have a Backout Plan:**
   - A plan to revert to the previous state if the change fails or causes issues.
   - Ensures a way to get back to where you started.

7. **Document the Changes:**
   - Record what was modified for future reference and transparency.

## Onboarding
### Definition
- The formal process of bringing a new person (new hire or transfer) into the organization.

### Security-related Procedures During Onboarding:
1. **Sign IT Agreements:**
   - Includes employee handbook or a separate Acceptable Use Policy (AUP).
   - Ensures the new hire understands and agrees to IT security policies.

2. **Create Accounts:**
   - Login credentials for network access.
   - Associate the user with proper groups and departments.
   - Assign correct rights and permissions using the principle of least privilege.

3. **Provide Required IT Hardware:**
   - Laptops, tablets, etc.
   - Devices should be preconfigured and ready to go with security measures in place.

## Offboarding
### Definition
- The formal process for when a user leaves the organization.

### Importance
- This process should be pre-planned, not decided ad-hoc, to prevent security risks.

### Security-related Procedures During Offboarding:
1. **What Happens to the Hardware?**
   - Collection, inventory, and secure sanitization or repurposing.

2. **What Happens to the Data?**
   - Backup, transfer to a supervisor, or secure deletion.
   
3. **Account Information is Usually Deactivated but Not Always Deleted:**
   - **Deactivation:** Prevents immediate access but retains the account.
   - **Reason for deactivation vs. deletion:** Allows retrieval of encrypted files, decryption keys, or other important data linked to the account if needed later. Deleting immediately could lead to irreversible data loss.

## Playbooks
### Definition
- Conditional, step-by-step sets of processes and procedures to follow in response to specific events or scenarios.

### Purpose
- Provides a broad, structured approach for handling incidents or routine complex tasks.

### Examples:
- Investigate a data breach.
- Recover from ransomware.

### Format
- Can be a manual checklist.

### Automation
- Can be used to create automated activities.

### Integration
- Often integrated with **SOAR platform** (Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response).
   - **SOAR Function:** Integrates third-party tools and data sources.
   - **SOAR Benefit:** Makes security teams more effective by automating mundane tasks, allowing them to focus on more critical work.

## Monitoring and Revision
### Core Principle
- IT security is constantly changing, so processes and procedures must also change.

### Reasons for Continuous Monitoring and Revision:
1. **Update Security Posture:**
   - Implement tighter change control.
   - Create additional playbooks as needed.
   - Acquire new technologies.
   - Continuously improve overall security.

2. **Change to an Individual Procedure:**
   - Update existing playbooks.
   - Include additional checks or steps.

3. **New Security Concerns:**
   - Protect against emerging threats (attackers constantly find new ways to exploit vulnerabilities).
   - Ensure playbooks and processes account for these new threats.

## Governance Structures
### Boards
- A panel of specialists, such as the Board of Directors.
- Sets broad tasks or requirements for committees (strategic objectives).

### Committees
- Composed of subject-matter experts.
- Considers input from the board.
- Determines next steps for a topic at hand (tactical implementation).
- Presents results back to the board for approval or further feedback.

### Government Entities
- Operate differently from private organizations.
- Driven by legal concerns, administrative requirements, and political issues.
- Often open to the public (transparency is key).

### Centralized vs. Decentralized Governance
- **Centralized Governance:** The source of processes and procedures is located in one location with a group of decision-makers (e.g., a single executive team).
- **Decentralized Governance:** Spreads the decision-making process around to other individuals or locations (e.g., department heads, regional managers).

- No single "right" or "wrong" approach; the best fit depends on the organization's structure and needs.

## Takeaways for Exam
1. **Formal Procedures are Crucial:**
   - Understand that security procedures like change management, onboarding, and offboarding are formal processes designed to reduce risk, ensure consistency, and maintain a strong security posture.
2. **Change Management Cycle:**
   - Memorize the key steps: Scope -> Risk Analysis -> Plan -> Approval -> CCB Review -> Backout Plan -> Documentation.
   - Emphasize the role of the Change Control Board (CCB) and the necessity of a backout plan for rollback if the change fails.
3. **Onboarding Security:**
   - Focus on signing IT agreements, especially Acceptable Use Policy (AUP), creating accounts with least privilege, and providing secure hardware.
4. **Offboarding Security (Critical):**
   - Recognize that offboarding must be pre-planned. Key actions include hardware retrieval, data handling (backup/transfer), and deactivating accounts to preserve decryption keys or data needed later.
5. **Playbooks for Incident Response:**
   - Understand playbooks as conditional, step-by-step guides for specific events (e.g., data breach, ransomware).
   - Know their benefit in creating consistency and their integration with SOAR platforms for efficiency and automation.
6. **Continuous Improvement (Monitoring & Revision):**
   - IT security is dynamic; procedures must be constantly monitored and revised to adapt to new technologies, evolving threats, and changes in the organization's security posture.
7. **Governance Structures:**
   - Differentiate between strategic roles of boards (setting broad objectives) and tactical implementations by committees (subject-matter experts determining next steps).
   - Understand distinct considerations for government entities (legal, administrative, political, public transparency).
8. **Risk Mitigation:**
   - Throughout all these procedures, the underlying goal is always to mitigate security risks, prevent unauthorized access, ensure business continuity, and maintain compliance.
