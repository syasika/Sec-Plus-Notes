# Security Policies

## 1. Security Policy Guidelines

### Overview
- **Primary Goal:** Provide **CIA (Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability)**.
    - **Confidentiality:** Protecting information from unauthorized access.
    - **Integrity:** Ensuring information is accurate and has not been tampered with.
    - **Availability:** Ensuring information and resources are accessible to authorized users when needed.

### Purpose of Policies
- **Establish Rules and Guidelines:** Define how everyone should act to maintain CIA.
- **Types of Policies:**
    - **High-level Strategies:** Broad goals, such as data storage requirements and security event procedures.
    - **Detailed Security Goals:** Specific rules for actions like appropriate Wi-Fi usage and remote access.

### Security Policies vs. Technical Controls
- **Security Policies:** Answer the "what" (what should be done) and "why" (why it should be done).
- **Technical Controls:** Answer the "how" (how to implement the policies).

## 2. Information Security Policies

### Definition
- **Comprehensive, Centralized Resource:** A single document containing all security-related policies for an organization.

### Key Aspects
- **Compliance Requirements:** Often mandated by regulations or industry standards, critical for avoiding legal issues and fines.
- **Detailed Security Procedures:** Outlines actions to take in various scenarios, such as discovering malware on a workstation, connecting to a VPN from home, or exploiting vulnerabilities.
- **Roles and Responsibilities:** Clearly defines who is responsible for what in security matters.

### Enforcement
- **Active Enforcement:** Policies are only useful when actively enforced by the organization. Effective enforcement ensures their effectiveness.

## 3. Acceptable Use Policies (AUP)

### Definition
- **Comprehensive Policy:** Defines acceptable use of company assets and resources, such as computers, internet, telephones, and mobile devices.

### Documentation
- **Included in Rules of Behavior:** Often part of broader documents outlining acceptable usage guidelines.

### Legal Protection
- **Limits Liability:** Protects the organization from legal issues due to employee misuse.
- **Grounds for Dismissal:** Provides clear reasons for disciplinary action if policies are violated, important for legal defense.

## 4. Business Continuity

### Concept
- **Planning for Disruptions:** Preparing for situations where business operations may be disrupted by unforeseen events.

### Reliance on Technology
- **Modern Businesses:** Rely heavily on computer systems and technology, making them vulnerable to disruptions.

### Alternative Methods
- **Manual Transactions:** Using paper receipts instead of digital POS.
- **Phone Calls:** Approving transactions over the phone when primary systems are unavailable.

### Crucial Point
- **Documentation and Testing Before a Problem Occurs:** Ensures plans are effective and understood in advance of a crisis.

## 5. Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP)

### Definition
- **Formal Set of Policies:** Focuses on recovering IT systems and infrastructure after a disaster, either natural or man-made.

### Relationship to BCP
- **Component of BCP:** Part of broader Business Continuity Plan, specifically addressing technical recovery aspects.

### Goal
- **Keep Operations Running:** Maintain business operations or restore them as quickly as possible.

### Types of Disasters
- **Natural Disasters:** Tornadoes, hurricanes, floods, earthquakes.
- **Technology Failures:** Hardware failures, network outages.
- **Human Created Disasters:** Cyberattacks, insider threats, accidental deletions.

### Comprehensive Plan Elements
- **Recovery Location:** Alternate physical site for operations.
- **Data Recovery Method:** Procedures for restoring lost data from backups.
- **Application Restoration:** Steps to bring applications back online.
- **IT Team and Employee Availability:** Plans for staff mobilization and accessibility.

## 6. Security Incidents

### Definition
- **Security Violations:** Events that compromise the integrity, confidentiality, or availability of information systems.

### Examples
- **User Clicks Malicious Email Attachment:** Executes malware communicating with external servers.
- **DDoS Attack:** Renders systems unavailable.
- **Confidential Information Theft:** Data exfiltration for ransom or public release.

### Importance of Policies
- **Detailed Policies:** Determine the "next steps" for handling each type of incident.

## 7. Incident Response Roles

### Definition
- **Specialized Teams:** Trained to handle security incidents and ensure swift, effective response.

### Key Roles
- **Incident Response Team:** Specialized group trained and tested for handling security events.
- **IT Security Management:** Provides corporate support, resources, and overall direction.
- **Compliance Officers:** Ensure all incident handling procedures comply with relevant regulations.
- **Technical Staff:** Hands-on resolution of technical issues.
- **User Community:** First to observe unusual activity, providing initial information.

## 8. NIST SP800-61

### Full Name
- **National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) Special Publication 800-61 Revision 2: "Computer Security Incident Handling Guide"**

### Purpose
- **Structured Framework:** Provides a systematic approach for incident response.

### The Incident Response Lifecycle
1. **Preparation:** Proactive measures before an incident occurs.
2. **Detection and Analysis:** Identifying the scope and nature of an incident.
3. **Containment, Eradication, and Recovery:** Managing the impact and restoring systems.
4. **Post-incident Activity:** Lessons learned, documentation, and policy updates.

## 9. Software Development Lifecycle (SDLC)

### Definition
- **Structured Process:** A method for developing software applications efficiently.

### Goal
- **Efficient Development:** Moving from initial idea to deployed and maintained application on schedule and within budget.

### Framework Importance
- **Structure Management:** Provides a framework for managing complex projects with many moving parts and customer requirements.

### Popular SDLC Models

#### Waterfall Model
- **Linear, Sequential Approach:** Each phase must be completed before the next.
    - **Phases:** Requirements -> Design -> Develop -> Test -> Deploy -> Maintenance.
    - **Pros:** Clear structure, easy to manage for well-defined projects.
    - **Cons:** Less flexible, difficult to incorporate changes late in the cycle.

#### Agile Model
- **Iterative and Incremental Approach:** Focused on continuous feedback and rapid delivery.
    - **Cycles:** Requirements -> Design -> Develop -> Test -> Deploy -> Review (repeatedly).
    - **Pros:** Highly adaptable to change, customer collaboration, faster delivery of features.
    - **Cons:** Can be less predictable in terms of final scope/cost, requires active client involvement.

## 10. Change Management

### Definition
- **Formal Process:** Controlling and managing changes to an organization's IT infrastructure, applications, or configurations.

### Purpose
- **Minimize Negative Impact:** Reduce the risk of changes affecting operations and security.

### Examples of Changes
- **Software Upgrades**
- **Firewall Configuration Modifications**
- **Switch Port Changes**

### Common Risk
- **Change Management Overlooked:** Often ignored, leading to significant negative impacts.

### Clear Policies Essential
- **Frequency:** How often changes are scheduled.
- **Duration:** Estimated time for implementation.
- **Installation Process:** Detailed steps for applying the change.
- **Fallback Procedures:** Steps to revert if the change fails or causes issues.

### Implementation Challenge
- **Difficult Culture Shift:** Implementing an effective change management process can be challenging, requiring a shift in organizational culture.

# Key Takeaways for Exam

1. **CIA Triad is Fundamental:** Always link security policies back to Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability.
2. **Policies vs. Controls:** Policies define *what* and *why*, while controls define *how*. This distinction is critical.
3. **Information Security Policies are the Master Document:** Understand their role as a centralized resource for all security-related processes, compliance, procedures, and roles.
4. **AUP Protects the Organization:** Know that an Acceptable Use Policy defines appropriate use of company assets and serves to limit legal liability and provide grounds for disciplinary action.
5. **BCP vs. DRP:**
    - **Business Continuity Plan (BCP):** Focuses on maintaining business operations during and after a disruption, often involving non-IT aspects.
    - **Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP):** A component of BCP, specifically addressing the recovery of IT systems and infrastructure.
    - Both require documentation and testing before a problem occurs.

6. **Incident Response Lifecycle (NIST SP800-61):** Remember the four phases: Preparation, Detection & Analysis, Containment & Eradication & Recovery, Post-incident Activity. Understand the purpose of each phase.
7. **Incident Response Roles:** Be familiar with key players: Incident Response Team, IT Security Management, Compliance Officers, Technical Staff, and User Community.
8. **SDLC Models:** Understand when to use Waterfall (linear, sequential) vs. Agile (iterative, flexible).
9. **Change Management is Crucial Risk Mitigation:** Focus on making changes safely, understanding frequency, duration, installation process, and fallback procedures. Highlight the importance of clear policies and cultural shift.
