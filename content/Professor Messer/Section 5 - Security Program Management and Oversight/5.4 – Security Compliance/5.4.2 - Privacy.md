# Privacy Legal Implications

## Introduction to Privacy Legal Implications
- **Core Concept:** Privacy is a set of guidelines that govern the collection and use of personal data, constantly evolving due to technological advancements and societal concerns.
- **Universal Concern:** Everyone is concerned about privacy in today's digital age.
- **Organizational Data:** Organizations collect vast amounts of data, much of which is subject to various privacy laws.

## Levels of Privacy Legislation

### 1. Local/Regional Privacy Laws
- **Scope:** Set by state and local governments.
- **Examples of Protected Data:**
    - Legal information (e.g., property records)
    - Vehicle registration details
    - Medical licensing information

### 2. National Privacy Laws
- **Scope:** Apply to everyone within a specific country.
- **Examples:**
    - **HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act):** Protects health information in the USA.
    - **COPPA (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act):** Protects online privacy for children under 13 in the USA.
    - Other national laws that affect all citizens.

### 3. Global Privacy Laws
- **Scope:** Multiple countries working together to ensure privacy for their citizens, regardless of location.
- **Example:** **GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation):** A prime example of a global/regional privacy law.

# GDPR - General Data Protection Regulation

## Overview
- **Nature:** A European Union regulation.
- **Purpose:** Provides data protection and privacy for individuals in the EU.
- **Scope of Protection:** Covers a wide range of personal data, including:
    - Name, address, photo
    - Email address
    - Bank details
    - Posts on social networking websites
    - Medical information
    - A computer's IP address
    - Genetic makeup, physical characteristics, location data

## Key Rights and Controls under GDPR
- **Controls Export of Personal Data:** Users have significant control over their data.
- **User Decision-Making:** Users can decide where their data goes.
- **Right to be Forgotten:** Users can request the removal of their data from search engines and websites. If a website holds private information, the user can request its removal, and the website is legally obligated to comply.
- **"Data Subjects" Control:** Gives individuals (data subjects) control over their personal data.

# Data Subject

## Definition
- **Core Definition:** Any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person.
- **Simplified:** An individual with personal data.
- **Inclusive:** This includes everyone whose data is being collected or processed.

## Examples of Data Subject Information
- Name
- ID number
- Address information
- Genetic makeup
- Physical characteristics
- Location data
- Any other information that can identify an individual.

## Perspective Shift in Laws
- **GDPR's Innovation:** Privacy is defined from the perspective of the data subject, shifting control back to the individual.
- **Historical Context:** Many prior privacy laws placed responsibility on third-party organizations or companies, rather than empowering individuals. GDPR shifts this control back to the individual.

# Data Responsibilities

## High-Level Data Relationships
- **Focus:** Organizational responsibilities regarding data, often non-technical.

### 1. Data Owner
- **Role:** Accountable for specific data.
- **Position:** Often a senior officer within the organization.
- **Examples:**
    - **VP of Sales:** Owns customer relationship data.
    - **Treasurer:** Owns financial information.

# Data Roles

## Data Controller
- **Role:** Manages the purposes and means by which personal data is processed.
- **Decision-Maker:** Determines why and how data is handled.

## Data Processor
- **Role:** Processes data on behalf of the data controller.
- **Executor:** Carries out the processing as directed by the controller.
- **Nature:** Often a third-party organization or different internal groups.

## Example: Payroll Controller and Processor
- **Payroll Department (Data Controller):**
    - Defines payroll amounts and timeframes (the "purposes and means").
- **Payroll Company (Data Processor):**
    - Processes payroll and stores employee information (on behalf of the payroll department).
- **Privacy Implication:** Significant private and personal data is transferred between the controller and processor.
- **Protection:** Non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) are often used to ensure privacy when working with third-party data processors.

# Data Inventory and Retention

## Data Inventory
- **Question:** What data does your organization store?
- **Importance:** Documenting your data inventory is crucial.
- **Definition:** A listing of all managed data within an organization.
- **Key Details to Document:**
    - **Owner:** Who is responsible for this data?
    - **Update Frequency:** How often is the data modified or refreshed?
    - **Format of the Data:** What structure or type of data is it?

## Data Use Cases

### 1. Internal Use
- **Purpose:** Data used within the organization.
- **Examples:**
    - Project collaboration
    - IT security monitoring
    - Data quality checks

### 2. External Use
- **Purpose:** Data shared outside the organization.
- **Considerations:**
    - **Selection:** Carefully select which data to share publicly.
    - **Compliance:** Must follow existing laws and regulations regarding privacy.
- **Overall Importance:** Understanding your data inventory is crucial for knowing what type of data you have and ensuring any sharing aligns with legal guidelines for privacy.

## Takeaways for Exam

1. **Evolving Nature of Privacy:** Understand that privacy laws are not static and are constantly changing due to technological advancements and societal concerns.
2. **Multi-Layered Privacy Laws:** Recognize that privacy regulations exist at local/regional, national, and global levels, with examples for each (e.g., medical licensing at local, HIPAA at national, GDPR at global).
3. **GDPR Significance:**
    - Know it's a European Union regulation for data protection.
    - Be able to list types of personal data it protects (name, email, bank details, medical, IP address, genetic, location, etc.).
    - Understand the "Right to be Forgotten" and how it empowers data subjects.

4. **Data Subject Definition & Perspective:**
    - Define a "data subject" as an identified or identifiable natural person.
    - Emphasize that modern privacy laws (like GDPR) define privacy from the data subject's perspective, giving them control.

5. **Data Roles (Owner, Controller, Processor):**
    - **Data Owner:** High-level accountability, often a senior officer.
    - **Data Controller:** Determines the purposes and means of processing data.
    - **Data Processor:** Processes data on behalf of the controller.
    - Be able to differentiate these roles and provide an example (e.g., payroll department as controller, payroll company as processor).

6. **Importance of Data Inventory:**
    - Understand the necessity for organizations to document what data they store.
    - Know what a data inventory includes (owner, update frequency, format).
    - Recognize the different uses of data (internal vs. external) and the privacy considerations for each, especially adherence to laws when sharing externally.

7. **Legal Compliance:** Always connect data handling practices back to the legal and regulatory requirements for privacy. NDAs are a practical tool for ensuring compliance with third-party processors.
