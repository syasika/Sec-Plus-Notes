# Data Types and Classifications

## Introduction
Data is one of an organization's most important assets. Not all data is the same; its management changes based on who has it and what's being done with it.

---

## I. Data Types (What the Data Is)

### A. Regulated Data
- **Definition:** Data whose protection rules are set by a third-party, such as government laws or industry standards.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Managed by a third-party.
  - Subject to government laws and statutes.
- **Examples:**
  - **Credit card information**: Must comply with Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS).
  - **Government data**: Compliance with specific laws like HIPAA for health data or GDPR for personal data.

### B. Trade Secret
- **Definition:** An organization's confidential formulas, practices, designs, instruments, patterns, or compilations of information that give it a competitive economic advantage.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Unique to the organization.
  - High value; competitors would love to obtain them.
- **Security Implication:** Requires robust security to prevent disclosure.

### C. Intellectual Property (IP)
- **Definition:** Creations of the mind, such as inventions, literary and artistic works, designs, and symbols used in commerce.
- **Characteristics:**
  - May be publicly visible, like a published book or product design.
  - Protected by copyright and trademark laws.
- **Security Implication:** Protection often involves legal mechanisms rather than strict secrecy.

### D. Legal Information
- **Definition:** Data related to legal proceedings, regulations, and associated personal details.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Includes court records, documents, judge and attorney information.
  - Often contains personally identifiable information (PII) and other sensitive details.
- **Security Implication:** Challenges in balancing public accessibility with protecting private sensitive details.

### E. Financial Information
- **Definition:** Data pertaining to monetary transactions, assets, and liabilities.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Internal company financial details (e.g., balance sheets, profit/loss).
  - Customer financials (e.g., account balances, transaction history).
  - Payment records (e.g., credit card data, bank records).
- **Security Implication:** Highly sensitive; requires stringent protection against unauthorized access and modification.

### F. Human-readable Data
- **Definition:** Data that can be easily understood and interpreted by humans without special tools or processing.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Humans can understand the data directly.
  - Very clear and obvious.
- **Examples:** A written document, a spreadsheet with clear labels.

### G. Non-human readable Data
- **Definition:** Data that is not easily understood by humans in its raw form and requires specific tools or systems for interpretation.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Not easily understood by humans.
  - Encoded data.
- **Examples:** Barcodes, images (raw pixel data), encrypted data.

### H. Hybrid Data Formats
- **Definition:** Formats that combine both human-readable and non-human-readable elements.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Some formats are a hybrid, allowing both human and machine interpretation.
- **Examples:** A barcode with numerical digits printed below it, CSV, XML, JSON files (structured for machines but often designed to be somewhat human-readable).

## II. Classifying Sensitive Data (How We Categorize Data for Protection)

### A. Levels of Categorization
- **Key Principle:** Not all data has the same level of categorization/sensitivity.
- **Example:** License plate numbers (often public) vs. health records (highly private).
- **Impact:** Different levels of sensitivity require different security and handling measures:
  - Additional permissions (Access Control).
  - A different process to view (Workflow/Procedures).
  - Restricted network access (Network Segmentation).

### B. Data Classifications

#### 1. Proprietary
- **Definition:** Data that is the exclusive property of an organization.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Belongs solely to the organization.
  - May also include trade secrets.
- **Example:** Coca-Cola's secret formula.

#### 2. PII - Personally Identifiable Information (PII)
- **Definition:** Data that can be used to identify an individual.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Directly or indirectly links information to a specific person.
- **Examples:**
  - Name, date of birth.
  - Mother's maiden name.
  - Biometric information (fingerprints, facial scans).
  - Address, phone number, social security number.

#### 3. PHI - Protected Health Information (PHI)
- **Definition:** Health information associated with an individual.
- **Characteristics:**
  - A specific type of PII related to health.
  - Protected by regulations like HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act).
- **Examples:**
  - Health status, health care records.
  - Payments for healthcare.
  - Any demographic information that can be used to identify the individual and relates to their health.

#### 4. Sensitive (Broad Classification)
- **Definition:** A general category for data that requires protection due to its nature.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Includes intellectual property, PII, and PHI.
  - Requires controls to prevent unauthorized access or disclosure.

#### 5. Confidential
- **Definition:** Very sensitive data that must be approved to view.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Higher level of sensitivity than just "sensitive."
  - Access is typically restricted and requires explicit authorization.
- **Example:** Internal financial statements, unreleased product designs.

#### 6. Public / Unclassified
- **Definition:** Data with no restrictions on viewing.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Anyone should be able to view it.
  - Generally poses no risk if disclosed.
- **Example:** Company press releases, marketing materials.

#### 7. Private / Classified / Restricted
- **Definition:** Data with restricted access, often requiring formal agreements.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Restricted access.
  - May require a Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) to view.
  - Similar to "Confidential" but often used in government/military contexts for varying levels of secrecy.

#### 8. Critical
- **Definition:** Data that should always be available.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Focuses on the availability aspect of the CIA Triad.
  - Disruption to this data would severely impact operations.
- **Security Implication:** Requires robust processes and procedures to maintain uptime and accessibility (e.g., backups, redundancy, disaster recovery plans).

## Key Concepts for Exam

- **Data is an Asset:** Understand its fundamental value.
- **Distinction between "Data Types" and "Data Classifications":**
  - **Types:** What kind of information it is (e.g., financial, legal, health).
  - **Classifications:** How sensitive or important it is, dictating protection levels (e.g., public, confidential, critical).
- **Regulated Data:** Focus on external compliance (PCI DSS, HIPAA).
- **Trade Secrets vs. Intellectual Property:**
  - **Trade Secrets:** Value from secrecy and uniqueness.
  - **Intellectual Property:** Value from ownership and legal protection (copyright, trademark), even if public.
- **PII & PHI:** Crucial definitions and examples. PHI is a subset of PII.
- **Human-readable vs. Non-human readable:** Understand the difference in how data is consumed.
- **Impact of Classification:** Know that different classifications lead to different security measures (permissions, processes, network access, NDAs).
- **Critical Data:** Emphasizes availability in the CIA Triad.

## Exam Tips

1. **Definitions:** Be able to define each data type and classification clearly.
2. **Examples:** Be able to provide concrete examples for each data type/classification.
3. **Distinctions:** Understand the subtle differences between similar terms (e.g., Trade Secret vs. Intellectual Property, Sensitive vs. Confidential vs. Private).
4. **Security Implications:** For each type/classification, consider why it needs protection and how that protection might differ (e.g., legal protection for IP, secrecy for trade secrets, availability for critical data).
5. **Acronyms:** Memorize PII and PHI.
6. **Context:** Understand that classification isn't just about the data itself but also the context in which it's used and stored.
