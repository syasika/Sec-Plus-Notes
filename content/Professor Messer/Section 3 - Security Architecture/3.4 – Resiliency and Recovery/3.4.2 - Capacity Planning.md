# Capacity Planning

## Introduction to Capacity Planning
Capacity planning is the ongoing challenge in IT to predict how much capacity is needed for any particular service. The core goal is to match supply with demand, but this isn't always an obvious equation.

### Consequences of Imbalance
#### Under-provisioning (Too Little Capacity)
- **Application Slowdowns**
- **Outages**
  - *Result:* Poor user experience and potential business loss.

#### Over-provisioning (Too Much Capacity)
- **Paying Too Much** (wasted resources, unnecessary expenditure).
  - *Result:* Reduced profitability and inefficient resource allocation.

### Balanced Approach
To achieve a balanced approach:
- Add the right amount of people.
- Apply appropriate technology.
- Build the best infrastructure.

## People Aspect of Capacity Planning
Some services inherently require human intervention. For example, call center support lines and manual configurations in technology services.

### Too Few Employees
- **Need to recruit new staff** (hiring, onboarding, training).
  - *Result:* Time-consuming process with associated costs (recruitment, salaries, benefits).

### Too Many Employees
- **Redeploy** employees to other parts of the organization.
- **Downsize** (layoffs, terminations).
  - *Key Takeaway:* Humans are a difficult resource to easily ramp up or down due to time, cost, and ethical considerations.

## Technology Aspect of Capacity Planning
### Scalability is Key
Scalable technology allows for easy growth and shrinkage. Not all services can scale easily; capacity planning must be integrated into the early stages of engineering/design.

#### Web Services
- **Strategy:** Distribute the load across multiple web services using load balancers.
  - *Scaling:* Increase or decrease the number of web servers behind the load balancer, often invisibly to users.

#### Database Services
- **Strategy 1:** Cluster multiple SQL servers for redundancy and load distribution.
- **Strategy 2:** Split (shard) the database into smaller pieces, distributing them across multiple servers to increase capacity and performance.

#### Cloud Services
- **Advantage:** On-demand services with seemingly unlimited resources as long as you pay.
  - *Cost Implication:* More usage means more cost. Flexible scaling requires careful cost management.

## Infrastructure Aspect of Capacity Planning
### Underlying Framework
Infrastructure refers to the physical and virtual components supporting applications and services, such as application servers, network services, CPU, network bandwidth, and storage.

#### Physical Devices (On-Premise Data Center)
- **Process:** Purchase, configure, and install.
- **Challenges:**
  - **Acquisition:** Ordering, shipping, receiving.
  - **Deployment:** Unboxing, configuring, installing in racks, cabling.
  - **Testing & Production:** Testing, then deploying to production.
  - *Conclusion:* Slow and labor-intensive to scale.

#### Cloud-Based Devices
- **Advantage:** Easier and faster deployment.
  - **Process:** Click a few buttons on a cloud-based console to create a new application instance.
- **Benefits:**
  - **Rapid Scaling:** Easily ramp up when demand is high.
  - **Right-Sizing:** Easily scale down (or "right-size") when demand decreases, reducing costs.
  - **Useful for unexpected capacity changes.**
  - *Conclusion:* Offers flexibility and agility for capacity adjustments.

## 🧠 Takeaways for Exam Prep
1. **Core Definition & Goal:**
   - **Capacity Planning** ensures you have enough resources (supply) to meet anticipated user/system needs (demand) without overspending.
   - The primary goal is to balance supply and demand.

2. **Consequences of Imbalance:**
   - **Under-provisioning:** Leads to performance issues and service outages, impacting user satisfaction and business operations.
   - **Over-provisioning:** Results in wasted financial resources (paying for unused people, technology, or infrastructure).

3. **The Three Pillars of Capacity Planning:**
   - **People:** Human resources.
   - **Technology:** Software, applications, databases.
   - **Infrastructure:** Hardware, networking, storage (physical or cloud).

4. **Scaling Challenges & Solutions for Each Pillar:**
   - **People:**
     - **Challenge:** Humans are the hardest and slowest resource to scale up or down.
     - **Exam Tip:** Understand that human capital management is a significant part of capacity planning, not just IT systems.
   - **Technology:**
     - **Challenge:** Needs to be designed for scalability from the outset. Legacy systems might be difficult to scale.
     - **Solutions:**
       - **Web Services:** Use load balancing and multiple instances to distribute traffic.
       - **Database Services:** Employ clustering (multiple servers for the same database) and sharding (splitting data across databases).
       - **Cloud Services:** Leverage on-demand resources for automatic or rapid scaling, but be mindful of variable costs.
   - **Infrastructure:**
     - **Challenge:** Differs significantly between physical (on-premise) and cloud environments.
     - **Physical (On-Premise):** Requires manual purchase, configuration, and installation. Slow, involves lead times, and is capital-intensive. Difficult for unexpected spikes.
     - **Cloud-Based:** Offers rapid deployment and scaling (both up and down). Ideal for fluctuating demand and unexpected capacity changes as it's operational expenditure driven.

5. **Strategic Importance:**
   - Capacity planning is crucial for maintaining service levels, cost optimization, business continuity, and future growth by planning for anticipated increases in demand.
