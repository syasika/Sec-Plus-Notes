# Power Resiliency

## Introduction to Power Resiliency

**Foundation of Technology:** Power is the essential resource that connects all technologies and forms the backbone of modern operations. Ensuring a reliable power supply is crucial for continuous operation.

**Importance of Planning:** It's vital to properly engineer and plan for power outages to ensure uninterrupted service. Most data centers and organizations do not generate their own power, relying instead on third-party utility providers who cannot be controlled.

**Mitigation Strategies:** Different strategies are employed based on the duration of power issues:
- **Short Power Outages (Minutes to Hours):**
- **Long-Term Power Issues (Hours to Days):** Such as those caused by natural disasters.

---

## Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS)

**Purpose:** Provides short-term backup power to protect against various power issues.
**Protection Against:**
- **Blackouts:** Complete loss of power.
- **Brownouts:** Significant drop in voltage (under-voltage).
- **Surges:** Excessive amount of voltage (over-voltage).

**Types of UPS:**

### a. Offline/Standby UPS
- **Operation:** Operates primarily on main power.
- **Switchover:** Switches to battery backup when the main power fails, with a brief delay.
- **Cost:** Generally lower cost.
- **Use Case:** Suitable for systems that can tolerate a momentary power interruption.

### b. Line-Interactive UPS
- **Operation:** Constantly monitors incoming voltage and adjusts as needed.
- **Switchover:** Switches to battery backup during complete blackouts, offering better protection against voltage fluctuations than standby UPS.
- **Use Case:** Good for areas with frequent voltage sags/brownouts.

### c. On-Line/Double-conversion UPS
- **Operation:** Always supplies power from the battery.
- **Conversion:** Converts incoming AC power to DC to charge the battery and then back to AC for connected devices.
- **Switchover:** No switchover time during a power failure, ensuring continuous operation.
- **Cost:** Most expensive type.
- **Use Case:** Critical systems where any interruption is unacceptable (e.g., data centers, medical equipment).

**Key Features:**
- **Auto Shutdown:** Can signal connected systems to perform a graceful shutdown before battery depletion.
- **Battery Capacity:** Varies by model and determines the desired runtime for connected devices.
- **Outlets:** Provides multiple power outlets, with some surge-protected only and others battery-backed.
- **Phone Line Suppression:** Additional protection against surges in Ethernet or telephone cables.

---

## Generators

**Purpose:** Provides long-term backup power to ensure continuous operation during extended outages.
**Requirement:** Requires fuel storage (e.g., diesel, natural gas) for operation.
**Capacity:** Can be sized to power an entire building or specific sections of outlets within a building marked as "generator-powered."

**Startup Process:**
- Takes several minutes to start up and reach full operating speed (ramp-up time).
- During this period, there is a power gap.

**Combined Solution (UPS + Generator):**
- Organizations often use both UPS and generators for comprehensive resiliency.
- The UPS covers the short initial power gap while the generator starts up.
- Once running, the generator takes over to provide long-term power, ensuring continuous supply during the transition from utility power to generator power.

## Exam Takeaways

1. **Define Power Resiliency:** Understand its importance for technology infrastructure and the challenges of relying on third-party power.
2. **Differentiate UPS vs. Generators:**
   - **UPS:** Short-term backup, protects against blackouts, brownouts, surges; instant or no switchover (online).
   - **Generators:** Long-term backup requiring fuel, takes time to start up.

3. **Know the Three UPS Types and Their Distinctions:**
   - **Offline/Standby:** Main power -> switch to battery (brief delay).
   - **Line-Interactive:** Main power with voltage regulation -> switch to battery (brief delay). Key feature: handles brownouts by boosting voltage.
   - **On-line/Double-conversion:** Always on battery, no switchover. Cleanest power.

4. **Understand UPS Features:** Know the importance of auto shutdown for graceful system shutdown, battery capacity, and surge protection for data/phone lines.
5. **Generator Considerations:**
   - **Fuel:** Essential for operation.
   - **Ramp-up Time:** A critical vulnerability during startup.
   - **Synergy with UPS:** Understand why a UPS is used in conjunction with a generator (to bridge the startup gap).
6. **Power Quality Issues:** Be able to define and distinguish between:
   - **Blackout:** Complete power loss.
   - **Brownout:** Voltage drop.
   - **Surge:** Voltage spike.
   - Identify which devices protect against which issues.
