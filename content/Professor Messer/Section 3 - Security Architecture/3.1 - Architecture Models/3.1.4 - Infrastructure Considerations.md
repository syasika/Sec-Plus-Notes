# Infrastructure Considerations

## Availability
- **Definition**: The ability of a system to be operational and accessible when needed. Ensures users can access data, complete transactions, and use application capabilities.
- **Foundation of IT Security**: Without availability, other security measures like confidentiality and integrity are meaningless.
- **Balancing Act with Security**: Systems must be available but only to the right people. Significant investments in ensuring uptime include monitoring systems for performance and setting up redundant components to prevent single points of failure.
- **Important Metric**: Often evaluated by system uptime (e.g., 99% or "five nines" 99.999%).

## Resilience
- **Definition**: The ability of a system to maintain availability and recover quickly after an unexpected event or outage.
- **Key Questions**:
  - Can you keep the system available during an event?
  - How fast can you recover?
- **Factors Affecting Recovery**:
  - **Root Cause Analysis**: Understanding why outages occur.
  - **Replacement Hardware Installation**: Time to acquire and install new hardware.
  - **Software Patch Availability**: If a software bug caused the issue, is a fix available?
  - **Redundant Systems**: How quickly can failover or backups be restored?

## Cost
- **Everything Ultimately Comes Down to Cost**:
  - **Initial Installation**: Varies significantly across platforms and technologies.
  - **Ongoing Maintenance**: Annual costs for software licenses, hardware support, and personnel.
  - **Replacement or Repair Costs**: Costs associated with fixing or replacing failed components. May require multiple spares.
  - **Tax Implications**: Categorization of costs (e.g., operating expense vs. capital expense) affects taxes. Depreciation also plays a role.

## Responsiveness
- **Definition**: How quickly a system provides a response to a user's request. Measures the speed of information requests and responses, especially important for interactive applications.
- **Speed is Crucial**:
  - Slower responses can degrade user experience significantly.
  - All parts of the application contribute to overall responsiveness; any weak link can cause delays.

## Scalability
- **Definition**: How easily an infrastructure can increase or decrease capacity to handle varying workloads. This might happen many times a day, such as during peak usage periods.
- **Elasticity**: The ability to expand and contract resources dynamically based on demand.
- **Resource Challenge**: Building for maximum size is expensive; organizations often scale as needed, with security monitoring scaling alongside the system.

## Ease of Deployment
- **Definition**: How straightforward and efficient it is to set up and configure an application or service. Applications have many moving parts like web servers, databases, caching servers, firewalls, load balancers, etc.
- **Deployment Complexity**:
  - Involves managing hardware resources, cloud budgets, and adherence to change control procedures.
- **Automation**: Cloud-based deployments can use automation for rapid deployment of application instances.

## Risk Transference
- **Definition**: Minimizing risk by transferring it to a third party. Common methods include cybersecurity insurance.
- **Cybersecurity Insurance**:
  - **Covers Attacks and Downtime**: Provides financial coverage for cyber incidents, including ransomware recovery costs.
  - **Protects Against Legal Issues**: Helps cover the costs associated with legal proceedings after a security breach or service interruption.

## Ease of Recovery
- **Definition**: How quickly and easily a system can be restored to a functional state after a failure or disruption. Time is money; faster recoveries minimize financial impact and maintain availability.
- **Examples**:
  - Malware Infection: Reloading the operating system from original media takes about an hour, while reloading from a corporate image (pre-configured) only takes around ten minutes.

## Patch Availability
- **Definition**: The readiness and accessibility of software updates, bug fixes, and security patches.
- **Software Updates**: Essential for continuous security and stability. Ensure the latest version is running to mitigate known vulnerabilities.
- **Regular Updates**: Most companies have regular update schedules; test patches before deploying them in production.
- **Rare Patching**: Some companies rarely patch, which can significantly increase the risk of successful attacks.

## Inability to Patch
- **What if Patches Aren't an Option?**
  - **Embedded Systems**: Often not designed for regular updates (e.g., HVAC controls, time clocks).
  - **Security Controls**: If patching isn’t possible, implement additional layers of security around the device, such as firewalls.

## Power
- **Foundational Element**: Modern technology cannot function without electricity. Requires extensive engineering, especially for critical systems.
- **Overall Power Requirements**: Vary greatly depending on the environment (e.g., data centers vs. office buildings).
- **Primary and Backup Services**:
  - **Primary Power**: Usually supplied by utility providers.
  - **Backup Services**: Essential for maintaining availability during outages, including UPS (Uninterruptible Power Supply) and generators.

## Compute
- **Definition**: The processing power and resources (CPU, RAM) an application uses to perform its functions. Modern applications often require distributed computing.
- **Compute Engine**: Refers to the resources dedicated to processing data and running application logic.
- **Cloud Options**: Various instance types, serverless functions, and multiple CPUs across different clouds offer enhanced scalability and resilience.

# Exam Takeaways

1.  **CIA Triad and Availability**: Understand that availability is crucial for IT security. A system not available isn't useful, even if it has strong confidentiality and integrity.
2.  **Availability Metrics**: Define and understand the importance of system uptime (e.g., "five nines").
3.  **Resilience and Recovery**: Differentiate between being merely available and being resilient. Know that MTTR (Mean Time to Repair) is a key metric for resilience, and recovery time depends on various factors.
4.  **Comprehensive Cost**: Remember that cost includes initial installation, ongoing maintenance, potential replacement/repair costs, and tax implications.
5.  **Responsiveness & User Experience**: Understand why responsiveness is critical, especially in interactive applications, and how delays can negatively impact user experience.
6.  **Scalability & Elasticity**: Grasp the concepts of scalability and elasticity—quickly adjusting system capacity. Cloud environments excel in this area through automation but introduce management complexity.
7.  **Ease of Deployment & Automation**: Modern applications have many moving parts; ease of deployment is vital, often simplified by orchestration/automation (especially in cloud).
8.  **Risk Transference & Cyber Insurance**: Recognize cybersecurity insurance as a method to transfer risk. Understand what it covers and its growing importance.
9.  **Recovery Speed Matters**: Faster recovery methods are more efficient and less costly; design for ease of recovery.
10. **Patching is Non-Negotiable (Mostly)**: Regular patching is crucial for security and stability. Not patching increases the risk of successful attacks.
11. **Inability to Patch and Mitigations**: Be aware that some systems, especially embedded ones, cannot be easily patched; additional security controls are critical in these cases.
12. **Power & Compute as Foundational**: Recognize power and compute resources as fundamental infrastructure elements. Understand the need for robust power infrastructure (primary providers, UPS, generators) and how compute resources drive application performance and scalability.
