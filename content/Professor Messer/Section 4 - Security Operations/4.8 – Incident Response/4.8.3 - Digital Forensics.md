# Digital Forensics

## Core Concepts of Digital Forensics

Digital forensics involves collecting and protecting information related to security intrusions or events. The main goals are not only to understand what happened but also to preserve evidence for potential legal proceedings.

- **Primary Goals:**
  - Understand the details of a security event.
  - Develop better protections for the future.
  - Collect evidence that is admissible in legal proceedings.
- **Guiding Document:** [[RFC 3227]] - "Guidelines for Evidence Collection and Archiving" outlines best practices in the industry.
- **Standard Process:**
  1. **Acquisition:** Gathering the data.
  2. **Analysis:** Examining the collected data.
  3. **Reporting:** Documenting the findings.

### Critical Requirement
The process must be detail-oriented, with extensive notes essential for use in court years after the event.

## Legal Hold

A **Legal Hold** (or litigation hold) is a formal process to preserve relevant information when litigation is reasonably anticipated.

- **Definition:** A legal technique to ensure all forms of relevant information are preserved.
- **Initiation:** Initiated by **legal counsel**, not the IT or security department.
- **Process:**
  1. **Hold Notification:** Sent to **data custodians** (people who manage the data).
  2. **Data Preservation:** Custodians are instructed to preserve all relevant data.
  3. **Ongoing Obligation:** This creates an obligation to preserve the data until the hold is lifted.

### ESI (Electronically Stored Information)
- **Definition:** The target of the legal hold, including data from emails, documents, databases, and more.
- **Collection:** Collected and stored in a separate, secure repository to prevent modification or deletion.

## Chain of Custody

The **Chain of Custody** is crucial for ensuring the integrity of evidence. It involves a chronological documentation trail showing the seizure, custody, control, transfer, analysis, and disposition of evidence.

- **Purpose:** To control evidence and maintain its integrity.
- **Documentation:** Everyone who contacts or handles the evidence must be documented to prove that it hasn't been tampered with.
- **Digital Chain of Custody:**
  - **Hashes:** Cryptographic hashes (e.g., SHA-256) are used to create a digital fingerprint. A matching hash verifies the integrity.
  - **Digital Signatures:** Verify the identity of the person handling the data and ensure that it hasn't been altered.

### Process
1. Label and catalog every piece of evidence.
2. Digitally tag all items for ongoing documentation.
3. Securely seal and store physical evidence.

## The Forensic Process in Detail

### 4.1 Acquisition

This phase involves gathering data from all relevant sources without altering the original evidence.

- **Data Sources:**
  - **Disk:** Hard drives, SSDs.
  - **RAM:** Volatile memory (must be captured while the system is live).
  - **Firmware:** BIOS/UEFI.
  - **OS Files:** System files, logs, configuration files.
- **Distributed Data:** Evidence may be spread across:
  - Servers
  - Network devices (routers, switches)
  - Firewall logs
- **Virtual Systems:** For VMs, a **snapshot** is the best way to capture evidence. It contains all files, memory state, and information about the virtual machine at a specific point in time.
- **Artifacts:** Look for left-behind digital items that provide clues:
  - Log information
  - Recycle bins
  - Browser bookmarks and history
  - Saved logins and passwords

### 4.2 Reporting

The reporting phase involves documenting all findings clearly, concisely, and legally.

- **Key Components of a Forensic Report:**
  1. **Summary Information:** A high-level overview of the security event.
  2. **Detailed Explanation of Data Acquisition:** A step-by-step description of how, when, and where the data was collected.
  3. **The Findings:** A factual analysis of the data.
  4. **Conclusion:** Professional results and opinions based on the analysis.

### 4.3 Preservation

Preservation involves handling evidence correctly to ensure it remains unaltered and admissible in court.

- **Core Principle:** Isolate and protect the data to allow for future analysis without any alterations.
- **Best Practices:**
  - **Work from Copies:** Always create a bit-for-bit copy (forensic image) of the original evidence and perform analysis on the copy. Securely store the original.
  - **Manage Mobile Devices Carefully:** Isolate devices in Faraday bags to prevent remote wiping and quickly image them.
  - **Live Collection:** Capturing volatile data like RAM or data on encrypted drives is critical when the system can't be powered down.

## E-Discovery (Electronic Discovery)

E-discovery involves collecting, preparing, reviewing, interpreting, and producing electronic documents required by legal processes.

- **Definition:** The process of gathering and producing data for a legal case.
- **Key Differences from Forensics:**
  - E-discovery focuses on **gathering and producing** data. It does not generally involve analysis or determining intent.
  - Digital forensics is the *analysis* of that data to understand an event.

### How They Work Together
- The **e-discovery** process might obtain a storage drive, such as a USB drive.
- Forensic experts then analyze it, potentially recovering deleted data relevant to the case.

## Exam Takeaways & Key Concepts

- **Standard Three-Step Digital Forensics Process:**
  - 1. **Acquisition:** Obtain data.
  - 2. **Analysis:** Examine data.
  - 3. **Reporting:** Document findings.

- **Legal Hold:**
  - A directive from legal counsel to preserve all relevant data for impending litigation, creating an ongoing obligation for data custodians.

- **Critical Concept for Evidence Integrity:**
  - **Chain of Custody**: The documented trail proving the evidence hasn't been tampered with. Hashes are used to verify digital integrity.

- **Importance of "Live Collection":**
  - To capture volatile data (like RAM) and access data on encrypted systems that would become inaccessible if powered off.

- **Best Way to Acquire Data from a Virtual Machine:**
  - Take a **snapshot**. It captures the entire state of the VM (disk, memory, configuration).

- **Difference Between E-discovery and Digital Forensics:**
  - **E-discovery** collects and produces data for legal cases; digital forensics analyzes that data to understand an event.

- **RFC 3227:**
  - "Guidelines for Evidence Collection and Archiving," key document for digital forensic best practices.

- **Golden Rule of Preservation:**
  - Always work from copies (forensic images) never the original evidence. This preserves the original data in its pristine state.
