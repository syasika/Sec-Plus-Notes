# Multifactor Authentication (MFA)

## Introduction to Multifactor Authentication (MFA)
- **Definition**: The process of proving your identity by using two or more different methods (factors) of authentication.
- **Purpose**: To significantly enhance security by requiring more than one piece of evidence for access.
- **Examples of Methods**:
    - **Password**: A secret word, phrase, or string of characters that only the legitimate user should know and keep secret.
    - **Mobile Authenticator App (Generating a Code)**: An app on your phone that generates time-based one-time passwords (TOTP).
    - **GPS Location**: Your current geographical location.

## Authentication Factors
These are different types of login parameters used to verify identity. They can be categorized into four main types:

1.  **Something You Know (Cognitive Factor)**
2.  **Something You Have (Possession Factor)**
3.  **Something You Are (Inherence Factor)**
4.  **Somewhere You Are (Location Factor)**

### Something You Know (Cognitive Factor)
- This factor relies on information that only the legitimate user should know and can recall.
- **Examples**:
    - **Password**: A secret word, phrase, or string of characters.
      - Very common authentication factor.
      - Must be memorized and kept secret.
    - **PIN (Personal Identification Number)**: A 4-digit code for an ATM card, typically memorized by the user.
      - Not usually contained on the smart card or ATM card itself.
    - **Pattern**: A series of patterns completed on a device screen (e.g., Android unlock pattern).
      - Only known to the user.

### Something You Have (Possession Factor)
- This factor relies on a physical item that only the legitimate user should possess.
- **Examples**:
    - **Smart Card**: A physical card (e.g., ID card, credit card with a chip) that integrates with devices. Often requires a PIN for MFA.
      - Combines "something you have" with "something you know."
    - **USB Security Key**: A physical USB device containing a unique digital certificate specific to the user.
    - **Hardware or Software Tokens**:
        - **Hardware Token**: A small physical device (e.g., a fob) that displays a changing number.
        - **Software Token**: An app on a mobile phone (e.g., Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator) that generates time-based one-time passwords (TOTP).
    - **Your Phone (SMS)**: Receiving an SMS with a code to your phone. The phone itself is "something you have."

### Something You Are (Inherence Factor)
- This factor relies on unique biological characteristics of the user.
- **Examples**:
    - **Biometric Authentication**:
        - **Fingerprint**: Scanning a unique fingerprint.
        - **Iris Scan**: Scanning the unique patterns of the iris in the eye.
        - **Voice Print**: Analyzing unique vocal characteristics.
      - Usually stores a mathematical representation (template or hash) and not the actual image or recording, enhancing privacy.
    - **Mechanism**:
        - Difficult to change: You can change a password but you cannot change your fingerprint or iris pattern.
        - Not foolproof: Biometrics can sometimes be circumvented (e.g., using a high-quality fake fingerprint).
        - Specific situations: Often used in very specific, high-security situations and frequently combined with other factors for stronger authentication.

### Somewhere You Are (Location Factor)
- This factor relies on the physical location of the user or the device being used for authentication.
- **Purpose**: To provide a factor based on your current location, allowing transactions only if you are in a particular geography.
- **Examples**:
    - **IP Address**: Provides an idea of where someone might be located but can be spoofed or dynamically assigned. Less precise with IPv6 due to larger address spaces.
    - **Mobile Device Location Services (Geolocation)**: Utilizes GPS, Wi-Fi triangulation, or cellular tower triangulation to pinpoint location to a specific area.
      - Requires the device to be in a location that can receive GPS information or near an identified mobile/802.11 network.
      - More precise than IP addresses alone but still not perfect.

## Takeaways for Exam Preparation
1.  **Define MFA**: Understand that MFA uses multiple, different factors to verify identity, not just multiple instances of the same factor (e.g., two passwords is still single-factor).
2.  **Know the Four Main Factors**:
    - **Something you know**: Passwords, PINs, patterns.
      - Key: Memorized secret.
    - **Something you have**: Smart cards, USB security keys, hardware/software tokens (TOTP/HOTP), phone for SMS codes.
      - Key: Physical possession.
    - **Something you are**: Fingerprints, iris scans, voice prints.
      - Key: Unique biological characteristic.
    - **Somewhere you are**: IP addresses, GPS/mobile device location.
      - Key: Physical presence/location.
3.  **Biometric Nuances**:
    - Understand that biometrics store a mathematical representation (template/hash), not the raw data (e.g., actual fingerprint image).
    - Recognize that biometrics are difficult to change and are not foolproof, often requiring combination with other factors.
4.  **Location Factor Nuances**:
    - Understand the difference between IP addresses (less precise, especially with IPv6) and mobile device location services (more precise, relies on GPS/Wi-Fi/cellular).
    - Know that location factors can be combined for better accuracy.
5.  **Benefits of MFA**: Increased security, reduced risk from compromised single factors (e.g., stolen password alone is not enough).
6.  **Conceptual Understanding**: MFA aims to create layers of security, making it exponentially harder for unauthorized users to gain access even if one factor is compromised.
