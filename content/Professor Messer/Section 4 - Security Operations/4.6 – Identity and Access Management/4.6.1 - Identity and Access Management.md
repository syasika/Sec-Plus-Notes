# Understanding Identity and Access Management (IAM)

## Core Purpose of IAM

It's all about ensuring that **the right people have the right access to the right resources at the right time**. This is fundamental to security.

---

## Components of IAM

1. **Lifecycle Management**: 
   - Onboarding, offboarding, changes.
   
2. **Access Control**: 
   - Defines what you *can* do.
   
3. **Authentication**: 
   - Verifies who you *are*.
   
4. **Authorization**: 
   - Determines what you're *allowed* to do.
   
5. **Governance**: 
   - Tracks and ensures compliance.

---

## Continuous Process of Provisioning/De-provisioning

Provisioning and de-provisioning are not just initial setups but ongoing adjustments (promotions, transfers) and timely removal of access.

---

## Principle of Least Privilege

Users should only have the minimum access necessary for their job. This limits potential damage from compromised accounts or insider threats.

---

## Identity Proofing

It's about verifying that a user is genuinely who they claim to be, especially during account creation. The stages include:
- **Resolution**: Identifying the user.
- **Validation**: Confirming identity details.
- **Verification/Attestation**: Ensuring the user’s authenticity and authorization.

---

## Single Sign-On (SSO) for Convenience and Efficiency

One login, many resources. Understand its time limitations and infrastructure requirements.

---

## LDAP: Directories Management Protocol

It's the protocol for managing structured directories of network objects (users, computers, groups). Familiarize yourself with X.500 Distinguished Names and the Directory Information Tree concept.

### X.500 Distinguished Names
- Uses `attribute=value` pairs to uniquely identify objects.
- Order: Most specific first, moving towards less specific.
  
  Example:
  ```
  CN=WIDGETWEB, OU=Marketing, O=Widget, L=London, ST=London, C=GB, DC=widget, DC=com
  - Common Name (CN): Identifies the person or object.
  - Organizational Unit (OU): A unit or department within the organization.
  - Organization (O): The name of the organization.
  - Locality (L): City or area.
  - State (ST): State, province, or county.
  - Country (C): 2-character ISO code.
  - Domain Component (DC): Components of the object's domain.
  ```

### X.500 Directory Information Tree
- Builds a tree-like structure to organize network resources.

  Components:
  - **Root**: Top of the tree.
  - **Container Objects**: Branches that hold other objects.
    - Examples: Country (`C=GB`, `C=US`), Organization (`O=Widget`), Organizational Units (`OU=Marketing`, `OU=Accounts`, `OU=MIS`).
  - **Leaf Objects**: Endpoints representing individual resources.
    - Examples: Users, computers, printers, files.

## SAML for Third-Party Authentication

SAML allows a service provider to trust an identity provider (IdP) for user authentication. Be aware of its browser-centric flow and limitations with mobile apps.

### The SAML Authentication Flow
1. **Access Request**: User attempts to access an application URL on the Resource Server.
2. **Redirect to Auth Server**: The Resource Server sends a signed/encrypted SAML request and redirects the user's browser to the Authorization Server.
3. **User Login**: User logs in to the Authorization Server using their credentials.
4. **SAML Token Generation**: Upon successful authentication, the Authorization Server generates a SAML token (an XML document) and sends it back to the client.
5. **Token Presentation**: The client's browser presents this SAML token to the Resource Server.
6. **Access Granted**: The Resource Server verifies the SAML token, trusts the assertion from the Authorization Server, and grants access.

---

## OAuth: Application Authorization

OAuth is an authorization framework that allows a user to grant a third-party application limited access to their resources on another service without exposing their credentials. It's primarily an authorization framework, not an authentication protocol itself.

### Example (Zapier & Google Account)
- When Zapier wants to access your Google Drive files:
  - You log into Google (authentication).
  - Google asks you to authorize Zapier.
  - If you click "Allow," OAuth creates a secure link for Zapier to perform actions on your Google Drive.

---

## Federation: Extending Trust

Federation allows users to use their existing credentials from one organization to access resources in another. It provides SSO and more across different organizations.

### Process
1. **Trust Relationship**: Establishes trust between identity systems.
2. **Authentication/Authorization**: Users authenticate with a primary provider (e.g., Facebook, Twitter) which then asserts their identity and authorization to the federated network.

Example: Logging into a website using your Facebook or Google credentials.

---

## Interoperability

The ability of different authentication and authorization systems/protocols to communicate and work together seamlessly. The choice of IAM technologies is often determined by:
- **Existing Infrastructure**: What resources and systems are already in place?
- **New Application Needs**: Does the new application require OAuth for its authorization framework, necessitating an API for authentication?

### Strategic Importance
Interoperability is a critical part of a larger IAM strategy, considering current needs and future goals.

## Takeaways for Exam Preparation

1. **Understand IAM's Core Purpose**: Ensure you grasp the fundamental goal of ensuring correct access at the right time.
2. **Differentiate IAM Components**: Know each component's role and function.
3. **Provisioning/De-provisioning is a Continuous Process**: Understand that it involves ongoing adjustments and timely removals.
4. **Principle of Least Privilege**: Users should have minimum necessary access to minimize risks.
5. **Identity Proofing**: Verify user authenticity during account creation.
6. **SSO for Convenience and Efficiency**: Recognize its limitations and infrastructure requirements.
7. **LDAP for Directories**: Familiarize yourself with X.500 Distinguished Names and the Directory Information Tree.
8. **SAML for Third-Party Authentication**: Understand its flow and browser-centric nature.
9. **OAuth for Application Authorization (NOT Authentication)**: Distinctly understand OAuth's role in authorization.
10. **Federation Extends Trust**: Know how users can leverage existing credentials across different organizations.
11. **Interoperability is the Practical Challenge**: Ensure chosen IAM solutions can communicate and integrate effectively.
