# Securing Wireless and Mobile

## Site Surveys

### Purpose
- **Determine existing wireless landscape** for new installations or troubleshooting.
- **Understand performance** of a new/existing network.
- **Gain insights into nearby networks' potential impact** on signal strength.

### Key Steps/Components

#### Determine Existing Wireless Landscape
- **Sample the existing spectrum**: Measure 2.4GHz and 5GHz bands to understand current usage.

#### Identify Existing Access Points (APs)
- **List internal APs**: Controlled by your organization.
- **Identify external APs**: Controlled by others, like neighbors or other tenants.
- **Interference management**: Knowing these helps in planning around them.

#### Work Around Existing Frequencies
- **Optimize channel layouts**: Plan to minimize overlap and maximize performance by choosing the right channels for each AP.

#### Plan for Ongoing Site Surveys
- **Dynamic environments**: Regular surveys ensure continued optimal performance and security.
- **Heat maps**: Visualize signal strengths across an area (e.g., office floor plan).
  - **Strong signals** appear as yellow/red areas.
  - **Weak signals** appear as darker/bluer areas.

## Wireless Survey Tools

### Purpose
- Gather detailed information about wireless networks in your vicinity.

### Benefits
#### Signal Coverage Analysis
- **List SSIDs and BSSIDs**: MAC addresses of APs.
- **Channel, band (2.4GHz, 5GHz)**, security type (WPA2 Personal, Open).
- **Vendor and mode (802.11 b/g/n/ac)**.
- **Signal level** (SNR, Signal %, Avg, Max, Min dBm) and noise level.

#### Potential Interference Identification
- **Identify problem areas**: Track down sources of interference.
- **Detect crowded channels or non-Wi-Fi devices** that might cause issues.

### Types of Tools
- **Built-in tools**: Basic utilities in operating systems (e.g., Windows, macOS).
- **3rd-party tools**: Specialized software like NetSpot for comprehensive analysis and visualization.
- **Spectrum analyzers**: Beyond Wi-Fi specific tools; show all signals on a frequency band.

## Mobile Device Management (MDM)

### Definition
- Centralized management system for both company-owned and user-owned mobile devices.

### Key Functionality & Benefits
#### Centralized Management
- Administrators can manage all mobile devices from a single platform.
#### Specialized Functionality
- Tailored features for security and control:
  - **Application Policies**: Control which apps are installed/used.
  - **Data Security**: Define how data is stored and accessed.
  - **Camera Controls**: Enable/disable based on location or policy (e.g., disable in secure areas).
  - **Remote Control**: Remotely wipe or lock a device if lost or stolen.

#### Device Segmentation
- **Personal vs. Business Partitions**: Divide the device into separate sections to protect corporate data.
- **Selective Remote Wiping**: Erase only the business partition without affecting personal data.

#### Manage Access Control
- Force screen locks and PINs on single-user devices.
- Enforce password complexity and lockout policies.

## Bring Your Own Device (BYOD)

### Definition
- Employees bring their own mobile devices for both personal and work use.

### Employee Ownership
- The employee owns the device.

### Challenges/Security Concerns
#### Meeting Company Requirements
- Devices must comply with corporate security policies managed by MDM.
#### Difficulty to Secure
- **Home vs. Work Device**: Data blurs between personal and sensitive corporate data.
- **Data Protection**: Ensure corporate data is protected from unauthorized access, apps, or accidental deletion.
- **Device Lifecycle**: What happens when an employee sells, trades in, or upgrades their device? Policies for secure data wiping are critical.

## Corporate Owned, Personally Enabled (COPE)

### Definition
- The company buys and owns the mobile device but allows employees to use it for both corporate and personal purposes.

### Key Characteristics
- **Full Control by Organization**: Similar to company-owned laptops and desktops.
- **Information Protection**: Corporate policies dictate how data is protected.
- **Data Deletion**: Corporate data can be deleted at any time by the organization (often using MDM).
- **Segmentation**: Corporate data is often partitioned from personal data, allowing selective remote wiping.

### Choose Your Own Device (CYOD)
- Similar to COPE but with a pre-approved list of devices provided by the company.
- The device remains corporate-owned and personally enabled.

## Cellular Networks

### Mobile Devices
- "Cell" phones (e.g., 4G, 5G).

### Network Structure
- Land divided into cells covered by antennas using specific frequencies.

### Security Concerns
- **Traffic Monitoring**: Data can be intercepted.
- **Location Tracking**: Precise location tracking via constant communication with cell towers.
- **Worldwide Access**: Increased attack surface due to global accessibility.
- **Updates and Patches**: Crucial for maintaining security against known vulnerabilities.
- **Physical Security**: Devices are easily lost, stolen, or hidden.

## Wi-Fi

### Local Network Access
- Connect devices to local networks and the internet.

### Security Concerns
- **Data Capture**: Unencrypted networks (especially public ones) can expose data in transit.
  - **Mitigation**: Use encryption (WPA2/WPA3 for local networks, **VPN** for public Wi-Fi).
- **On-path Attack (Man-in-the-Middle)**: Attackers can intercept or modify data.
  - **Mitigation**: Use strong authentication and secure protocols (HTTPS).
- **Denial of Service (DoS)**: Interference disrupts service.
  - **Mitigation**: Proper channel planning, spectrum analysis.
- **Rogue Access Points**: Malicious APs can capture user data.

## Bluetooth

### High-speed Communication
- Creates a Personal Area Network (PAN) over short distances.
- Connects various devices like smartphones, headsets, and smartwatches.

### Security Best Practices
- **Do not connect to unknown devices**: Follow the formal pairing process to prevent unauthorized connections.
- **Disable Bluetooth when not in use**: Reduce attack surface.
- **Keep devices updated**: Patches address Bluetooth vulnerabilities.

# Takeaways for Exam Preparation

1. **Understand Why Each Measure is Needed**:
   - Know the purpose behind site surveys, MDM, and BYOD.
   
2. **Differentiate Similar Terms**:
   - **BYOD vs. COPE vs. CYOD**:
     - **BYOD**: Employee-owned, personal & work use, complex data wiping.
     - **COPE**: Company-owned, personal & work use, full company control.
     - **CYOD**: Company-owned, personal & work use, user chooses from approved list.

3. **Identify Common Mobile/Wireless Security Concerns**:
   - **Signal Interference**: Site surveys and spectrum analyzers help manage it.
   - **Unauthorized Access/Data Capture**: Use encryption (WPA2/WPA3), secure protocols, and VPs for Wi-Fi; formal pairing for Bluetooth.

4. **Understand the Role of MDM**:
   - Centralized control over mobile devices.
   - Enforces policies to protect data, especially with partitioning.

5. **Be Aware of Inherent Risks**:
   - **Wi-Fi**: Local network access, data capture, on-path attacks, DoS.
   - **Cellular Networks**: Global access, location tracking, traffic monitoring.
   - **Bluetooth**: Short-range, potential for unauthorized connections if pairing is ignored.

6. **Think About Lifecycle Management**:
   - Securely remove corporate data when devices are retired, sold, or traded in (MDM function).

7. **Prioritize Encryption**:
   - Crucial for Wi-Fi and cellular security; VPs vital on untrusted networks.
