# Introduction to Gap Analysis

## Overview
- **Definition**: A study comparing "where you are" (current state) with "where you want to be" (desired state/goal).
- **The 'Gap'**: The difference between the current and desired states.
- **Purpose in IT Security**:
    - Constantly performed to understand future security needs.
    - Helps identify what security measures are needed.

## Complexity
- **Process**: Simple to define, but complex to execute.
    - **Research**: Extensive research is required.
    - **Participants**: Involves numerous participants across the organization.
    - **Timeframe**: Can take weeks, months, or even years to complete.
    - **Tasks**: Includes emails, data gathering, technical research, and project planning.

## Choosing the Framework
- **Importance of a Baseline**: Establishing a known baseline is crucial before starting. This defines "where you want to be."
- **Types of Baselines**:
    - **Internal Set of Goals**: Organization-specific objectives.
    - **Formal Standards**:
        - **NIST Special Publication 800-171 Revision 2**: Focuses on protecting sensitive government information.
        - **ISO/IEC 27001**: An international standard for managing information security.
    - **Custom Baselines**: Created based on the organization's specific needs.

## Evaluate People and Processes
- **Focus**: Assessing both human and procedural aspects of security.
- **People Baseline (Employees)**:
    - **Formal Experience**: Evaluating their background in IT security.
    - **Current Training**: Understanding what security training they have received.
    - **Knowledge of Security Policies and Procedures**: Ensuring they understand and follow established rules.

- **Examine Current Processes**:
    - **Research Existing IT Systems**: Understanding how current systems operate.
    - **Evaluate Existing Security Policies**: Assessing how well current policies align with the chosen baseline. This includes checking if they reflect actual practices.

## Compare and Contrast (The Analysis Phase)
- **Evaluation**: Comparing existing systems against the chosen baseline.
    - **Identify Weaknesses**: Pinpointing areas where current security falls short of the baseline requirements.
    - **Identify Strengths**: Noting existing strengths that can be leveraged or expanded.

- **Detailed Analysis**:
    - **Broad Security Categories**: Start by examining broad categories such as Access Control, Incident Response.
    - **Breakdown**: Break down these categories into smaller, granular segments. For example, under "Access Control," consider individual controls like User Registration and De-registration, Privileged Access Management, etc.

## The Analysis and Report
- **Final Comparison**: Synthesizing all gathered information to compare detailed baseline objectives with the current state.
    - Provides a clear, comprehensive view of the organization's security posture.

- **Path to Goal**: Determining necessary steps to bridge identified gaps. This path includes:
    - **Time**: Project timelines.
    - **Money**: Budget for new tools, training, personnel.
    - **Change Control**: Implementing new policies, procedures, and technologies.

- **Gap Analysis Report**:
    - **Current State Description**: Detailed findings of the "where you are."
    - **Recommendations for Meeting the Baseline**: Specific actions to close gaps and reach the desired state.

## Visualization Tools
- **Heatmap Example**: A visual representation (e.g., a matrix table).
    - **Rows**: Security Requirements, such as Access Control, Awareness & Training, Risk Assessment.
    - **Columns**: Locations or different departments, systems, etc.
    - **Color-coding**:
        - **Green**: Relatively close to meeting the baseline.
        - **Yellow**: Needs some work.
        - **Red**: Significant work needed to meet the baseline.
    - **Prioritization**: The heatmap helps prioritize efforts, starting with red areas (highest risk/biggest gap), then yellow, and finally green.

## Key Takeaways for Exams
1. **Core Concept**: Gap analysis is fundamentally about identifying the difference between your *current security state* and your *desired security state*.
2. **Purpose is Proactive**: It's not just fixing problems but planning for future security needs and strategic improvement.
3. **Comprehensive Process**: It's a time-consuming, resource-intensive project involving extensive research, data gathering, and multiple stakeholders.
4. **Baseline is Crucial**: You must have a clear "where you want to be" (a baseline) before identifying gaps. This can include internal goals, formal standards, or custom baselines.
5. **Focus on People AND Processes**: Effective gap analysis evaluates both the human element and procedural/systemic elements.
6. **Granular Analysis**: Don't just look at broad categories; break down security requirements into smaller, actionable segments to identify specific weaknesses and areas for improvement.
7. **Output is Actionable**: The final report provides a clear picture of the current state, detailed pathways to the desired state, and specific recommendations requiring time, money, and robust change control.
8. **Visualization Tools**: Familiarize yourself with how results are presented, such as heatmaps, which clearly illustrate compliance status across different security requirements and organizational segments.
9. **Prioritization**: The heatmap's color-coding helps prioritize efforts by focusing on areas with the most significant deficiencies (red), then yellow, and finally green.
