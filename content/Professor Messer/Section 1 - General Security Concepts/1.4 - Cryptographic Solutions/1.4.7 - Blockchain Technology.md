# What is Blockchain?

## Definition
- A **distributed ledger**, primarily used to keep track of transactions.
- Anyone can see it (transparency).

### Decentralized Nature
- Everyone on the blockchain network maintains a copy of the ledger.
- Records and replicates to anyone and everyone participating.
- Once changes are recorded, they are distributed to all participants.

## Practical Applications
- Payment processing (e.g., cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin)
- Digital identification
- Supply chain monitoring
- Digital voting

## The Blockchain Process

### Step 1: Transaction Request
- A digital transaction is initiated.
- **Examples:** 
    - Transferring Bitcoins from one person to another.
    - Recording medical records.
    - Performing data backups.
    - Transferring house title information.

### Step 2: Transaction Sent for Verification
- The transaction information is sent to every computer (or node) in the decentralized network.
- All participating nodes verify the transaction's validity.

### Step 3: Block Creation and Hashing
- A **verified transaction** is added to a new block of data.
- This block contains other recently verified transactions.
- A **secure code (a hash)** is calculated for this new block.
    - The hash is derived from the data within the new block and the hash of the previous block in the chain.
    - This linking via hashes creates the "chain" and ensures data integrity.
    - The hash acts as a unique digital fingerprint for the block.

### Step 4: Block Distribution & Chain Update
- The newly created block (with its transactions and hash) is added to the end of the Blockchain.
- This updated chain is then distributed and replicated to all nodes in the network.
- The transaction is now complete and immutably recorded.

## Security and Immutability

### Integrity through Hashing
- If **any blocks are altered** (even a single transaction within a block), its unique hash will change.
- Because each block's hash is calculated partially from the previous block's hash, altering one block will cause all subsequent hashes in the chain to become invalid.

### Consensus Mechanism
- When an altered chain is detected because its hashes no longer match the expected sequence, the network's other nodes (which hold valid copies) will **reject the altered chain**.
- This consensus mechanism prevents tampering and ensures the integrity and trust of the ledger.
- The majority of the network must agree on the validity of the chain.

## Takeaways for Exam

### Core Concepts to Master
1. **Definition of Blockchain:** Understand it as a distributed, decentralized, and immutable ledger that records transactions.
2. **Key Characteristics:**
    - **Distributed:** No central authority; copies of the ledger are maintained by many participants (nodes).
    - **Decentralized:** Control is spread across the network, not held by one entity.
    - **Immutable:** Once a transaction is recorded and added to a block, it cannot be altered without invalidating the entire subsequent chain.
    - **Transparent:** All participants can view the ledger (though identities might be pseudonymous).
    - **Secure:** Achieved through cryptographic hashing and consensus mechanisms.
3. **Blockchain Process Flow:**
    - Transaction request.
    - Network verification by nodes.
    - Block creation with hashing (linking to previous block).
    - Block distribution and update across the network.
4. **Role of Hashing:**
    - Understand that a hash is a unique digital fingerprint of a block's data.
    - Each new block's hash incorporates the hash of the previous block, creating an unbreakable "chain."
    - This cryptographic linking is fundamental to blockchain’s security and immutability.
5. **Security Mechanism Against Tampering:**
    - Explain how altering a block changes its hash and invalidates all subsequent blocks.
    - Emphasize that the distributed nature means other nodes will have the correct version, leading to the rejection of the tampered chain. This is a form of consensus.
6. **Applications:** Be familiar with several practical applications beyond just cryptocurrency (e.g., supply chain, digital ID, voting) to demonstrate a broader understanding.
